CAP-2020's original design set a precise timeline: enabling state legislation passed and a DTE-partnered local pilot designed in 2021, the pilot program launching in 2022, program expansion and individual-participant recruitment in 2024, and the landfill solar site opening for community subscriptions in 2026, targeting 11 MW of community solar by 2030 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). A community solar pilot was planned for Year Two of A2ZERO, building on Year One work that included negotiating community solar agreements and winning a National Community Solar Partnership technical assistance grant ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). The pilot is intended to expand solar access to residents who cannot install rooftop solar, including renters and low-income households ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). By Year Three, the Solarize Ann Arbor program had facilitated installation of over 4 MW of rooftop solar through more than 540 new residential installations, while also educating thousands of residents about energy production, electrification, and energy waste reduction ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). Achieving 5 MW of rooftop solar through Solarize is identified as a Year Four priority ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).
