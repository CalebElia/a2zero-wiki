Geothermal districts were proposed in the A2Zero plan as a shared infrastructure approach to providing low-carbon heating and cooling across multiple buildings or neighborhoods, with district systems achieving greater efficiency and lower per-unit costs than individual building installations by sharing ground loop infrastructure ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The Geothermal Districts initiative advances shared low-carbon heating and cooling systems in Ann Arbor neighborhoods and districts ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

In Year Three, the City won a $580,000 planning grant from the U.S. Department of Energy to design a district geothermal loop in the Bryant neighborhood, advancing that site as a flagship decarbonization model ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, that work advanced into site-specific design, with the city reporting the start of design for a district geothermal system in the Bryant neighborhood through the DOE grant; the system was intended to cover at least 75% of the heating and cooling load for 262 households, the County Mental Health facility, Bryant Elementary School, the Bryant Community Center, and the city's public works facility ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year 5 priorities call for winning a federal grant to implement at least part of the district geothermal system in the Bryant neighborhood ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

The National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) also provided Clean Energy to Communities technical assistance to determine data needs and draft a request for proposals for a district geothermal study around Veterans Park ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). Additionally, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] continued to support geothermal studies and analysis at two future [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]] sites ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, the city also supported geothermal installation at the new affordable housing property at [[locations/121-catherine-street|121 Catherine Street]] and initiated a city-wide networked geothermal study to assess how geothermal systems could support decarbonization of heating and cooling citywide ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year 5 priorities call for advancing that city-wide geothermal feasibility study and a deployment strategy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).
