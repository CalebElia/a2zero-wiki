Geothermal districts were proposed in the A2Zero plan as a shared infrastructure approach to providing low-carbon heating and cooling across multiple buildings or neighborhoods, with district systems achieving greater efficiency and lower per-unit costs than individual building installations by sharing ground loop infrastructure ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The [[initiatives/geothermal-districts|Geothermal Districts]] initiative advances shared low-carbon heating and cooling systems in Ann Arbor neighborhoods and districts as a pathway to replace fossil-gas use in buildings, building on earlier planning for district and networked geothermal infrastructure in neighborhoods and affordable housing sites ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).

In Year Three, the City won a $580,000 planning grant from the [[actors/us-department-of-energy|U.S. Department of Energy]] to design a district geothermal loop in the Bryant neighborhood, advancing that site as a flagship decarbonization model ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, that work advanced into site-specific design, with the city reporting the start of design for a district geothermal system in the Bryant neighborhood through the DOE grant; the system was intended to cover at least 75% of the heating and cooling load for 262 households, the County Mental Health facility, Bryant Elementary School, the Bryant Community Center, and the city's public works facility ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In 2024, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] won an award from the [[actors/us-department-of-energy|U.S. Department of Energy]] to support building Michigan's first networked geothermal system; the project is designed to help 262 homes, a community center, and a local school transition completely away from fossil gas to geothermal, and the report describes it as a foundational component of the city's effort to decarbonize heating and cooling systems ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). In Year Five, OSI and collaborators won $10,000,000 to support deployment of a networked geothermal system in the Bryant neighborhood, adding implementation funding to the earlier Bryant planning and design work ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). Year 5 priorities call for winning a federal grant to implement at least part of the district geothermal system in the Bryant neighborhood ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

Beyond Bryant, the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) provided Clean Energy to Communities technical assistance to determine data needs and draft a request for proposals for a district geothermal study around Veterans Park ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] also continued to support geothermal studies and analysis at two future [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]] sites ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, the city supported geothermal installation at the new affordable housing property at [[locations/121-catherine-street|121 Catherine Street]] and initiated a city-wide networked geothermal study to assess how geothermal systems could support decarbonization of heating and cooling citywide ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Five, the report states that geothermal was integrated into the new affordable housing site on Catherine Street in collaboration with the [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]], and installed at the [[locations/bryant-neighborhood-community-center|Bryant Community Center]] as part of making that site the city's first living, learning laboratory of carbon neutrality ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). OSI additionally initiated an approximately 18-month city-wide geothermal feasibility analysis with [[actors/stantec|Stantec]] to inform the role geothermal can play in decarbonizing heating and cooling across the city ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).

Year 5 priorities call for advancing the city-wide geothermal feasibility study and a deployment strategy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year 6 priorities likewise call for advancing the city-wide geothermal feasibility study together with a strategy for deploying geothermal ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
