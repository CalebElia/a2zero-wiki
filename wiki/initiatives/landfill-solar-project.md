The Landfill Solar Project, also referred to as the Wheeler Center Landfill Solar Project and later the Wheeler Center Solar Park, was proposed in CAP 2020 as a utility-scale solar installation on the City of Ann Arbor's capped landfill, developed in partnership with [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]], with the goal of adding major local renewable generation to Ann Arbor's electricity supply ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The project was described as being developed on the City-owned capped landfill and in Pittsfield Township, targeting 24MW of installed capacity by 2023 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]), while later planning documents reported a planned capacity of 20 MW ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). When complete, the project would be one of the largest landfill solar installations in the nation ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). The installation is intended to offset roughly 80% of Ann Arbor's municipal energy usage and includes a pilot of floating solar panels on a retention pond, a portion of clean energy for Pittsfield Township's municipal operations, and pollinator habitat co-benefits ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

By Year Three of A2ZERO, the project remained a named priority and was identified for groundbreaking as a Year Four priority targeted for 2024, as part of the broader effort to power the community's electric grid with 100% renewable energy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, the city reported that it continued strategizing and adjusting the plan to develop the Wheeler Center Solar Park using the landfill site for municipal solar installation ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). The Year Four report also states that Ann Arbor secured a $5,000,000 award to help advance solar at and around the former landfill site, indicating the project had been re-scoped beyond the original single-site concept ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). The project is also part of the broader ARPA-funded city facilities solar effort that includes installations at Wheeler, Water/Wastewater facilities, and the airport ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).

If demand exists and enabling legislation is passed, the project may be integrated into a community solar program open to public subscriptions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The estimated 10-year staffing cost is $80,000, with grid upgrade costs to be determined, and the greenhouse gas reduction potential is 23,000 metric tons CO₂e, or 1% of community-wide emissions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).
