The Sustaining Ann Arbor Together grant program serves as a vehicle for supporting community innovation and creativity related to advancing local sustainability and climate action, awarding funding to community groups working on sustainability-related activities ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). The program provides small grants to residents, schools, local nonprofits, and local businesses to advance sustainability-related activities in Ann Arbor's right of way, with collaborators including neighborhood associations, the [[actors/university-of-michigan|University of Michigan]], and [[actors/ann-arbor-public-schools|Ann Arbor Public Schools]], and an equity focus on helping underserved areas unlock capital to address their specific sustainability concerns ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The 10-year estimated cost is $1,000,000, and greenhouse gas reduction potential was not calculated for this initiative ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The program was continued during Year One of A2ZERO as part of the city's broader community engagement and equity strategy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). CAP-2020 set a goal of awarding at least 10 grants per year through the program ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). In Year Two of A2Zero (2021–2022), $100,000 was granted to community organizations through this program ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). More information is available at www.a2gov.org/sa2f ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). In Year Three, the program granted $70,000 to community organizations working on local sustainability and climate action ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Five, the program was featured as a discussion topic in a community speaker series hosted by the Ann Arbor District Library, alongside natural features, local food systems, and circular economy/waste reduction ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
