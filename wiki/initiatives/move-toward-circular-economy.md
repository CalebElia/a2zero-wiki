The Move Toward a Circular Economy initiative promotes resource reduction, material reuse, and regeneration across Ann Arbor, with the City aiming to create a model where goods and materials are recovered, reused, and recycled ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). Ann Arbor's circular economy work focuses on reducing waste and extending the useful life of materials through reuse-oriented systems and community engagement ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Key actions include promoting repair centers for bicycles and appliances, supporting toy, book, and tool libraries to decrease new purchases, and contracting with a private collector for weekly curbside textile collection from single-family homes with potential future expansion to multi-family properties ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The initiative also involves working with peer municipalities to create tools for transparency, traceability, and calculation of embedded greenhouse gas emissions for different materials and goods ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

By Year Four of A2ZERO implementation, this work had expanded from earlier education and mapping efforts into implementation support and formal planning, including a new three-year contract with [[actors/zerowaste-org|ZeroWaste.Org]] to promote circular economy activities and engagement throughout the community ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). The City also launched a circular economy planning initiative in coordination with a local resident advisory commission, with the goal of creating a circular economy development strategy within the next 12 to 18 months ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). As part of this work, the circular economy GIS map was updated to highlight businesses and organizations in Ann Arbor advancing circular economy practices ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year 5 priorities call for creating a circular economy strategy co-designed with a wide variety of Ann Arbor residents ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

Lower-income residents are identified as potential equity beneficiaries through access to repurposed and refurbished items; the estimated 10-year cost is $270,000 with greenhouse gas reduction potential not yet calculated ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).
