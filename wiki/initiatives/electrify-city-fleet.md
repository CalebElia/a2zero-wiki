The [[initiatives/electrify-city-fleet|Electrify City Fleet]] initiative advances replacement of fossil-fueled municipal vehicles with electric alternatives across Ann Arbor's operations. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]) It was authorized by City Council Resolution R-17-237 in June 2017, which directed the City Administrator to update the Green Fleets Policy and promote plug-in electric vehicle purchases ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The Green Fleets Team was tasked with developing a plan to electrify the City fleet by 2025, prioritizing light-duty vehicles first given the broader EV market availability, before addressing construction equipment and medium- and heavy-duty vehicles ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The remaining 10% of the fleet that cannot be electrified by 2025 consists of medium- and heavy-duty vehicles and specialized equipment ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

The 10-year estimated cost is $4,000,000, with a GHG reduction potential of 1,100 MTCO2e (0.05% of community-wide emissions), with all reductions expected to be achieved by 2026 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

In Year Four of A2ZERO implementation, Ann Arbor reported achieving 25% electrification of its light-duty fleet vehicles and set a goal of reaching 50% electrification of light-duty fleet vehicles by the end of calendar year 2024 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year Four additions also included purchase of the State of Michigan's first all-electric refuse trucks, procurement of two e-cargo bikes for the parks department, procurement of four additional e-bikes for the general fleet, and a state grant award to secure an electric forklift for city operations ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

In Year Five, the city reported an electric-vehicle adoption rate of more than 40% in its light-duty fleet and nearly 30% electrification across the total city fleet ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). The same year, the city also purchased two more electric refuse trucks, bringing the total on order to four, while the first electric refuse truck entered service in spring 2025 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
