title: Green Rental Housing Program
type: initiative

The Green Rental Housing Program integrates energy efficiency requirements into the City's existing rental licensing and inspection process, ensuring every rental unit in Ann Arbor meets a minimum energy efficiency performance standard while adding standards designed to promote safety, comfort, health, and efficiency citywide, including appliance electrification requirements ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). It is a planned A2Zero initiative focused on improving energy performance in rental properties and addressing a significant gap in the city's building decarbonization strategy, since rental properties house a large share of Ann Arbor residents who have limited ability to make energy improvements independently ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).

Rental units make up 55% of Ann Arbor's housing stock, making the program critical to community-wide GHG reductions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The program is intended to lower carbon emissions from rental properties, standardize quality expectations, lower energy costs for tenants, and provide resources for landlords, including trainings, rebates, and financing support ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]) ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The program assumes 80% compliance after four years in effect and no net increase in average rents outside of normal market inflation within five years of policy adoption ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The 10-year cost is estimated at $530,000; GHG reduction potential is incorporated into other plan calculations rather than reported separately ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

In Year Two, staff examined model cities, participated in regional cohorts, and facilitated a task force of tenants, landlords, engineers, architects, and low-income representatives to develop requirements tailored for Ann Arbor ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). The draft initiative was in review as of Year Two and expected to proceed to City Council in Year Three, with finalization identified as a Year 3 priority in the A2Zero Year 2 Annual Report ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). As of Year Three, the program's formal launch was retargeted as a Year Four priority ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). By Year Four, the City reported plans to bring the program to City Council for consideration in summer or fall 2024 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). After these earlier task-force development efforts and delays in bringing the program forward, Year 5 priorities called for creating the program and supporting local property owners in making substantial rental-efficiency improvements, and Year 6 priorities likewise call for creating a new Green Rental Housing program and supporting local property owners in making substantial rental-efficiency improvements ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
