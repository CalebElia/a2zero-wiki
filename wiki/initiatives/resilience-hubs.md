The A2Zero plan identifies development and leveraging of existing infrastructure as resilience hubs as a key preparedness action, including activating some homes and community spaces as shelters with resources and energy, identifying safe gathering points in neighborhoods during power outages and extreme heat or cold events, and using neighborhood needs mapping to inform hub placement and services ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|Resilience Hubs]] initiative grows from this call to use community spaces and neighborhood assets as preparedness sites during outages and extreme weather, and has developed into a network of community-serving facilities that support emergency response, emergency preparedness, resilience education, climate resilience, and distributed energy deployment ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]).

The initiative establishes community centers as designated sites for emergency preparedness and climate resilience support for Ann Arbor residents ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). In Year Two, the first resilience hub was launched at the [[locations/northside-community-center|Northside Community Center]], while designs for a second hub at [[locations/bryant-neighborhood-community-center|Bryant Community Center]] and a third at the [[locations/ann-arbor-senior-center|Ann Arbor Senior Center]] were completed for Year 3 implementation ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). The city also deployed over 200 emergency preparedness kits to residents and staffed an emergency preparedness shelter during a 5-day power outage in August 2021 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]).

By Year Three, the city completed and established [[locations/bryant-neighborhood-community-center|Bryant Community Center]] as its second resilience hub, and also applied for an EPA grant to formally launch a resilience network and expand hubs throughout Washtenaw County ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Creating Ann Arbor's third and fourth resilience hubs was identified as a Year Four priority and remained a Year Five priority ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Four, the city identified [[locations/peace-neighborhood-center|Peace Neighborhood Center]] and [[locations/green-baxter-court-community-center|Green Baxter Court Community Center]] as the next two hub sites, with initial engagement and planning underway ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year Four resilience work also added backup-power capacity through purchase of a mobile nanogrid, expanded solar-and-storage capacity at Bryant Community Center, and paired hub development with a new EPA-funded regional resilience network effort intended to expand hubs across Ann Arbor and Washtenaw County ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

In Year Five, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] hosted a Climate Teach-In breakout session on the role of resilience hubs in the climate transition, and in partnership with the [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]] issued a request for proposals for solar and battery storage installation at [[locations/green-baxter-court-community-center|Green Baxter Court Resilience Hub]] to meet community-identified needs ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
