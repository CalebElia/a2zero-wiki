During Year One of A2ZERO, Ann Arbor drafted a commercial and multi-family benchmarking ordinance as well as a home energy disclosure ordinance, with input sourced from stakeholder engagement ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). These ordinances aim to increase transparency around building energy performance and drive efficiency improvements across the city's building stock ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]).

The Commercial Benchmarking Ordinance is Michigan's first such ordinance, passed during Year Two of A2Zero, requiring large commercial and multifamily buildings throughout Ann Arbor to track and report on building energy and water performance ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). The first reporting deadline was June 1, and the resulting data is intended to increase awareness and practice of energy and water efficiency among operators and occupants of Ann Arbor's largest buildings ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). Benchmarking and transparency is anticipated to lead to average energy savings of 2–3% each year ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]).

By Year 3, the commercial energy and water benchmarking ordinance was fully implemented, making Ann Arbor the first city in the State of Michigan to enact such a requirement ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). A companion Home Energy Rating Disclosure policy for residential home sales was also fully drafted during Year 3, with introduction targeted for summer 2023 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).
