The A2ZERO Ambassadors Program was conceived in the CAP 2020 as a mechanism to rapidly scale community-wide carbon neutrality work by training residents to serve as peer educators and project leaders in their neighborhoods ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The program was also discussed at a planning meeting on December 4, 2019, focused on how to disseminate information about A2Zero and recruit community members to serve as neighborhood-level climate advocates, functioning as a grassroots outreach mechanism to extend A2Zero's reach into Ann Arbor neighborhoods beyond formal public events ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The plan also proposes activating neighborhood climate captains to support local resilience efforts and community cohesion, with complementary actions including activating neighborhood welcome ambassadors and developing a program to recognize neighborhood-level climate and resilience efforts ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). Youth participation through actions, videos, ambassadors, curriculum, training, and competitions is likewise identified as a component of community engagement ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

[[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] designed the program to partner with nonprofits including [[actors/community-action-network|Community Action Network]], [[actors/neutral-zone|Neutral Zone]], [[actors/united-way-of-washtenaw-county|United Way of Washtenaw County]], and [[actors/ann-arbor-public-schools|Ann Arbor Public Schools]] to train stipended cohorts of low-income adults and youth from underserved communities as Climate Ambassadors ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The original ten-year plan targeted 100 trained ambassadors by 2025 covering energy, water, waste, transportation, and local food efforts at an estimated cost of $350,000 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The program trains community members to serve as informed advocates and educators for A2Zero climate action initiatives ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) and as peer educators and local climate-action leaders ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

In Year One, 22 community members were trained as A2ZERO Ambassadors, and they designed and implemented seven sustainability-focused community projects while the program also developed educational materials for ambassadors to use in their neighborhoods ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). By the end of Year Two, the 2nd and 3rd cohorts had been completed, bringing the total number of trained ambassadors to over 50 individuals ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). By Year Three, the program had grown to 85 members, with ambassadors initiating work to increase composting rates through deep engagement activities and distribution of compost carts to residents; Year Four reporting also summarizes Year Three neighborhood-based engagement as including composting outreach, swap days, and other activities ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). A $229,000 AmeriCorps grant was also secured to bring 10 AmeriCorps members to [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] in support of local sustainability-related engagement, education, and the program ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).

In Year Four, the city graduated its 5th cohort to reach 99 trained ambassadors, then launched the 6th cohort and marked its 100th ambassador trained, effectively meeting the long-stated training milestone ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year Four also added science communication training in partnership with [[actors/relate-um|R.E.L.A.T.E. (UM)]], and ambassadors helped launch [[initiatives/green-energy-neighbors|Green Energy Neighbors]] and lead neighborhood resilience work through the [[initiatives/nourishing-neighborhood-pilot|Nourishing Neighborhood Pilot]] ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Completing the next two rounds of ambassadors to bring the total to over 100 trained ambassadors had been identified as a Year Four priority in the prior annual report ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). Year 5 priorities call for launching a youth A2ZERO Ambassador program and for achieving the ambassador goals outlined in A2ZERO ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

In Year Five, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] reported 118 total ambassadors trained, hosted 10 recruitment events at libraries, houses of worship, and University of Michigan student groups for the seventh cohort, published a StoryMap highlighting ambassador project impacts, and convened an annual Ambassador Summit for updates and peer sharing ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). The seventh cohort began training in early 2025 with graduation slated for June, which the report says would bring the total number of trained ambassadors to 148 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
