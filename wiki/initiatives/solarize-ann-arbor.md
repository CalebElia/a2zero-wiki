Solarize Ann Arbor is Ann Arbor's group-purchasing, bulk-purchase rooftop solar program, created to lower upfront installation costs for residents while expanding local renewable generation, community outreach, and broader resident participation in clean energy adoption ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). In its first year the program installed approximately 1.3 MW of solar on over 200 residential roofs, saving participants over $650,000 in upfront costs ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). Year Two added another 1.7 MW, bringing the cumulative total to over 3 MW across more than 430 households, representing over $1.5 million in upfront savings and an estimated $15 million in lifetime energy savings; the program also functions as an entry point for introducing residents to electrification and efficiency concepts ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). By Year Three, the program had reached 4 MW of cumulative solar installed, adding 540 new residential installations during the year ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In calendar year 2023, the program supported installation of 231 solar systems totaling over 1.8 MW, the highest number of Solarize installations in a single calendar year, bringing cumulative deployment to over 4.8 MW and more than 630 households served ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year Four reports that the 2023 cohort alone generated more than $296,000 in upfront cost savings and an estimated $2.4 million in 25-year energy cost savings, while the Year Five solar dashboard reports that the Solarize program helped residents save over $400,000 in upfront costs in 2023 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). By Year Five, the program had reached 5.4 MW of solar installed through the program, with 711 Solarize installations reported in Washtenaw County and projected energy-cost savings of more than $3.8 million over 25 years ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). The Year Five solar dashboard also states that more than 6.5 MW of solar has been installed in Ann Arbor since adoption of the A2ZERO Plan, and that annual solar installations in Ann Arbor peaked at 258 in 2023 across both Solarize and non-Solarize projects ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).

OSI developed a Solarize Toolkit in Year One — a step-by-step replication guide distributed through the [[actors/great-lakes-renewable-energy-association|Great Lakes Renewable Energy Association (GLREA)]] to help other communities adopt the bulk-purchase model beyond Washtenaw County ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). By Year Five, the program had expanded beyond Ann Arbor to other parts of Michigan including Grand Rapids ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). In Year Two, OSI initiated design of a [[initiatives/commercial-solarize-pilot|Commercial Solarize Pilot]] in collaboration with the [[actors/ann-arbor-washtenaw-2030-district|2030 District]], extending the model to businesses for the first time ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). OSI also published guidance and a sample policy to expand solar access in multi-unit developments and homeowner associations ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

The Year 5 priorities set a target of achieving 5.5 MW of solar installed through the program ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). The program's long-term target is 78 MW of local generation by 2030, projected to reduce 85,000 MTCO₂e (3.9%) with UM participation; the Year Five solar dashboard likewise states that Ann Arbor's A2ZERO Plan goal is to install 78 MW of new local onsite renewable energy generation by 2030 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
