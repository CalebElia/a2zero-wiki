The Sustainable Energy Utility (SEU) is Ann Arbor's proposed and now voter-authorized community-owned local energy utility model under A2Zero's renewable grid strategy, intended to provide electricity generated through local solar and battery storage systems installed on homes and businesses throughout Ann Arbor and to deliver 100% clean, reliable, locally built, and affordable electricity, described as built by the community for the community. It is presented as an opt-in, supplemental local utility model intended to provide clean, 100% renewable power to city residents, and it has been pursued alongside traditional municipalization of the electric grid as one of two parallel pathways under active exploration for achieving 100% renewable electricity. The SEU is rooted in Strategy 1's goal of a 100% renewable, reliable, and affordable electric grid for Ann Arbor by 2030. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

A formal proposal for the SEU was developed in Year Two, and the City opened a public engagement portal at www.a2gov.org/a2seu for residents to learn more and register their input. The SEU proposal was awarded the Project of the Year by the [[actors/great-lakes-renewable-energy-association|Great Lakes Renewable Energy Association]]. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]])

In Year Three, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] commissioned an Energy Options Analysis to determine the financial viability of the SEU, traditional municipalization, and other options for meeting community-wide renewable energy goals. A grant application was also submitted to the U.S. Department of Energy to formally create the SEU, with decisions expected in mid-2024. A strategy was expected to be brought to Council for formal consideration by the end of Year Four. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])

In Year Four, the city conducted a technical study and rate analysis for the SEU and launched a community education and engagement initiative describing it as an optional, supplemental, and community-owned utility authorized to deploy renewable energy in Ann Arbor. Voters were scheduled to decide in November 2024 whether the City should formally create the SEU. In the Year 5 priorities, the City identified support for education, engagement, and the potential launch of a local Ann Arbor Sustainable Energy Utility as a next-step priority. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

On November 5, 2024, Ann Arbor voters overwhelmingly authorized creation of the SEU with 79% of the vote, moving the initiative from proposal and education work into formal authorization. The Year Five report presents the SEU as one of the city's major clean-energy milestones and situates it within a broader transition that also includes state clean-energy law and local geothermal deployment. It also states that work immediately began on launch activities expected to take about 24 months after the vote. Year Five reporting adds that the city launched an SEU waitlist form, began sending nearly biweekly development updates to people on the waitlist, and secured $5,000,000 to support foundational tools, structures, and preliminary staffing needed to bring the SEU to fruition. In Year 6 priorities, the city says it will stand up the SEU and move significantly closer to the utility formally providing its first service offerings. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
