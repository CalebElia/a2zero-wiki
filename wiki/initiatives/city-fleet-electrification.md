Ann Arbor's City Fleet Electrification initiative aims to transition municipal vehicles from fossil fuels to electric power. A 2017 City Council resolution (R-17-237), predating A2Zero, directed the City Administrator to revive and update the Green Fleets Policy, promoting plug-in electric vehicle purchases and incorporating best practices from peer municipalities ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) — the origin of this initiative.

In Year One, the city transitioned 10% of its light duty fleet to electric, including making its first EV purchase for the Police Department ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). Year Two plans included commissioning the city's first all-electric refuse truck ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]), and by Year Two the city achieved a 20% fleet electrification rate for municipal vehicles and won awards to bring two electric refuse trucks into the City's fleet ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). By Year Four, electrification had reached 25% of the City's light-duty fleet, with a Year Five priority target of 70% ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). By Year Five, the total city fleet (not limited to light-duty vehicles) had reached nearly 30% electric ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
