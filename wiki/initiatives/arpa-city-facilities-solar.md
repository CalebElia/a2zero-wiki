This initiative supports solar and battery storage installations on critical City of Ann Arbor facilities, funded through the American Rescue Plan Act ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). A total of $4,500,000 in ARPA funding was secured for this purpose ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]), and the city also secured $2.5 million from the federal government to advance additional solar and energy storage installations ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Ann Arbor's city-facilities solar work focuses on deploying or planning solar installations across municipal properties ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

A request for proposals was released and vendors Enerlogics and Melink Solar were selected for installation of roughly 3 MW of solar on municipal sites including [[locations/wheeler-service-center|Wheeler Service Center]], the Water and Wastewater Treatment Plants, and the [[locations/ann-arbor-municipal-airport|municipal airport]], with construction set to begin fall 2023 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). A separate contract was executed for nearly 1 MW of solar at various Parks facilities, with installations underway as of Year Three ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, the city reported installation of over 250 kW of rooftop and ground-mounted solar at [[locations/leslie-park-golf-course|Leslie Park Golf Course]], [[locations/huron-hills-golf-course|Huron Hills Golf Course]], [[locations/cobblestone-farm|Cobblestone Farm]], [[locations/buhr-park|Buhr Park]], [[locations/ann-arbor-senior-center|Ann Arbor Senior Center]], [[locations/gallup-canoe-livery|Gallup Canoe Livery]], and [[locations/fuller-park|Fuller Park]] as part of its city-site solar deployment work ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

Installing at least 4 MW of solar at City facilities, including multiple energy storage systems, was identified as a Year 4 priority ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). By Year Four, active design work was underway for nearly 4 MW of additional solar at [[locations/ann-arbor-municipal-airport|Municipal Airport]], [[locations/ann-arbor-municipal-center|Ann Arbor Municipal Center]], [[locations/wheeler-service-center|Wheeler Service Center]], [[locations/water-resource-recovery-facility|Water Resource Recovery Facility]], [[locations/veterans-memorial-park|Veterans Memorial Park]], [[locations/fire-station-3|Fire Station 3]], [[locations/bicentennial-park|Bicentennial Park]], and [[locations/fire-station-6|Fire Station 6]], with deployment expected in Year 5 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). The city also reported work on a 500 kW battery at the Water Recovery Facility paired with rooftop solar ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Year 5 priorities call for installing solar or having a plan to install solar at every viable city facility ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).

Additionally, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] released a joint RFP with the [[actors/university-of-michigan|University of Michigan]] to add an additional 1 MW of solar to City facilities and up to 20 MW at University sites ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).
