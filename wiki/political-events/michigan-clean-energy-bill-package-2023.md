In late 2023, Governor Gretchen Whitmer signed a series of Michigan clean energy bills into law that changed the statewide electricity policy landscape relevant to Ann Arbor's A2Zero work. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) The Year Four report says Ann Arbor supported the State's clean energy bill package and describes it as putting Michigan on a pathway to 100% clean electricity by 2040. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) The report specifically identifies SB 271 as requiring the State of Michigan to generate 50% of its total electricity from clean energy sources by 2030 and 100% by 2040. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) Ann Arbor's Year Four report presents these laws as a notable shift in the clean energy landscape and as part of the broader context for local renewable-energy work, including local renewable purchasing and installation efforts by institutions, businesses, and residents, community choice aggregation legislation, and other grid-transition efforts. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])
