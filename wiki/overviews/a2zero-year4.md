This document is the fourth annual progress report on [[initiatives/a2zero-carbon-neutrality-plan|A2ZERO]], covering work completed from July 1, 2023 through June 3, 2024. Produced by the [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|Office of Sustainability and Innovations]] for the [[actors/city-of-ann-arbor|City of Ann Arbor]], it presents both a progress update and a public invitation to participate in the city's effort to achieve community-wide carbon neutrality by 2030 (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

The report opens with a greenhouse-gas inventory summary that grounds the rest of the document in [[locations/ann-arbor|Ann Arbor]]'s emissions profile. It states that the city must reduce community-wide annual emissions from over 2.1 million metric tons of CO2e to zero by 2030. Electricity used in homes and businesses remains the largest source of emissions at 40%, while fossil gas used in buildings accounts for 28%. Taken together, buildings produce 69% of total emissions, compared with 29% from fossil fuel use in vehicles and about 2% from waste disposal and processing. The report also notes that in 2023 more than 50% of [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]]'s fuel mix was coal and 13% was renewable, while highlighting Michigan's new clean-energy law requiring 50% of the state's total electricity from clean energy sources by 2030 and 100% by 2040 (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

Structurally, the report is organized around the seven A2ZERO strategies and lists notable accomplishments under each. Across those sections, it documents expanded municipal solar deployment, new electrification and rebate programs, implementation of disclosure and benchmarking tools, transportation and land-use changes, circular-economy partnerships, resilience-hub expansion, and broader communications and engagement infrastructure. It also emphasizes the scale of collaboration behind A2ZERO, stating that more than 130 organizations, 100 A2ZERO Ambassadors, and nearly a dozen city departments are working alongside OSI (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

The introduction and closing sections make clear that the report is also meant to recruit participation. Residents are invited to sign up for the monthly newsletter, apply to become an A2ZERO Ambassador, join the [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Green Business Challenge]], or participate as Community Collaborators. The Year 5 priorities section then turns those invitations into a near-term agenda, including additional solar and EV charging, possible creation of a [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]], more [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|resilience hubs]], expanded neighborhood decarbonization, and new storytelling and youth-engagement efforts (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).
