This document is the fifth annual progress report on A2ZERO, covering work from June 1, 2024 through May 31, 2025. It was produced by the [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|Office of Sustainability and Innovations]] for the [[actors/city-of-ann-arbor|City of Ann Arbor]] and is written as both a status update and a public-facing account of milestones, partnerships, lessons learned, and invitations for broader participation in the city's effort to reach community-wide carbon neutrality by 2030. The report repeatedly frames A2ZERO as a collective project involving residents, businesses, nonprofits, institutions, and city staff rather than a city-government-only program. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Its greenhouse-gas summary emphasizes both the scale of the challenge and a modest but important milestone: while final 2024 inventory data were still pending, the city reports that it has at least stopped emissions from increasing. The report also sharpens the emissions picture by identifying electricity use in homes and businesses as the largest source of local emissions at 39%, fossil gas use in buildings at 28%, transportation at about 30%, and waste disposal and processing at about 2%. Taken together, buildings account for 69% of community emissions, which helps explain why so much of the report centers on electricity supply, heating and cooling, and building upgrades. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

The report also situates local action within changing state and utility conditions. It notes that in 2024 over 40% of DTE's electric fuel mix was generated using coal, compared to 13% from renewables, while also highlighting Michigan's late-2023 clean-energy legislation, including SB 271, which requires 50% clean electricity by 2030 and 100% by 2040. Alongside that broader context, the report points to several major local milestones: Ann Arbor voters authorized creation of the Sustainable Energy Utility in November 2024, and OSI won a U.S. Department of Energy award to support what the report describes in the greenhouse-gas summary as the state's first networked geothermal system. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Structurally, the document is organized around seven strategy sections plus a year-six priorities section. The strategy sections are curated accomplishment lists rather than exhaustive program audits, so they are strongest at surfacing launches, grants, ordinances, partnerships, and visible implementation milestones. The year-six priorities section shows the city focusing on scaling delivery systems: standing up the Sustainable Energy Utility, expanding [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Green Business Challenge]] participation to 50 businesses, creating circular-economy and sustainable-food strategies, implementing a multifamily rebate program, securing funding to scale Aging in Place Efficiently, advancing geothermal and solar deployment, and significantly expanding household preparedness work. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
