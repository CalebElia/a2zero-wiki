## Foundation

Strategy 5 of CAP-2020 targets changing how [[locations/ann-arbor|Ann Arbor]] uses, reuses, and disposes of materials, with the six combined actions reducing baseline community-wide greenhouse gas emissions by 0.3% at a cost of just over $45,000,000 [Source: Page 85] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The dominant mechanisms are regulatory and programmatic tools including composting expansion, [[initiatives/commercial-recycling|commercial recycling enforcement]], construction and demolition waste ordinances, circular economy initiatives, dietary education, and refrigerant recycling. The six named actions comprising this strategy are: (1) Expand Composting Program, (2) Expand Commercial Recycling, (3) [[initiatives/require-sustainable-materials-in-developments|Require Sustainable Materials in New and Existing Developments]], (4) [[initiatives/move-toward-circular-economy|Move Toward a Circular Economy]], (5) [[initiatives/support-plant-rich-diet|Support a Plant Rich Diet]], and (6) [[initiatives/enhance-refrigerant-recycling-reuse-program|Enhance Refrigerant Recycling and Reuse Program]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). Notably, the emissions calculations cover only disposal-phase greenhouse gases; upstream emissions from extracting and producing goods are explicitly excluded from the quantified targets as of the plan's writing [Source: Page 85] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 5 had been described as the year of "circularity," centered on education, behavior change, and framework development. Year three included working sessions toward a [[initiatives/circular-economy-strategy|Circular Economy Strategy]], an updated circular-economy GIS map, a video series on local circularity stories, collaboration with [[actors/university-of-michigan|University of Michigan]] students on embodied carbon in buildings, the [[initiatives/plant-based-challenge|A2Zero Plant-Based Challenge]], a Local Food Festival, a drafted sustainable food framework, continued operation of the A2R3 returnable-container pilot, student waste-reduction efforts, [[initiatives/neighborhood-swap-days|neighborhood swap days]], compost outreach, and work with [[actors/ecology-center|The Ecology Center]] on broader resource-management education. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])

In year four, the strategy remained centered on voluntary participation and culture change, but with more formal delivery partners. OSI initiated three-year contracts with [[actors/zerowaste-org|ZeroWaste.Org]] to promote circular-economy activities and with [[actors/vegmichigan|VegMichigan]] to promote plant-forward diets and business engagement. In the first three quarters of the VegMichigan contract, more than 50 public events were held with upwards of 2,600 attendees; 529 residents participated in the 30-Day Plant-Based Pledge; and 93% of follow-up survey respondents said they would continue eating a more plant-based diet afterward. The city also launched the [[initiatives/sustainable-food-business-coalition|Ann Arbor Area Sustainable Food Business Coalition]], continued developing a strategy to scale the A2R3 returnable-container program, launched a circular-economy planning initiative with a resident advisory commission, and expanded operational waste work through commercial-recycling staffing support, compost-strategy development, Local Food Fest, move-in and move-out reuse efforts, and Neighborhood Swap Days. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

Year five makes this strategy look more formalized and measurable than in earlier reports. The city celebrated the third annual [[initiatives/ann-arbor-local-food-fest|Ann Arbor Local Food Fest]] with the [[locations/ann-arbor-farmers-market|Ann Arbor Farmers Market]] and [[actors/argus-farm-stop|Argus Farm Stop]], continued the A2ZERO Food Waste Challenge, and completed the first draft of the [[initiatives/ann-arbor-sustainable-food-framework|Ann Arbor Sustainable Food Framework]] for city-operated facilities and events. The food waste challenge engaged over 540 residents in June 2024, and VegMichigan and OSI reached over 1,700 residents, with more than 400 trying plant-based eating for the first time through the 30-day Plant-Based Pledge. The Ann Arbor Sustainable Food Business Coalition, now with over 40 businesses participating, completed its first full year exploring energy and water efficiency, food-waste prevention, and more sustainable packaging. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Circular-economy planning itself moved closer to a formal action agenda. OSI received grant funding to support creation of a formal circular economy action plan, established a Resident Advisor Committee that co-designed and implemented a resident survey to better understand current and desired needs and opportunities for a local circular economy, and released an RFP for firms to aid business engagement in circular-economy action planning. The city also supported the "More Life, Less Stuff" social marketing campaign, which compiles stories from community partners and residents about reducing waste and engaging more with the world around them. More concrete diversion programs continued as well: five Neighborhood Swap Day events and related community-led swaps kept over 10,000 items in reuse rather than landfill; three [[initiatives/electronics-collection-events|electronics collection events]] with [[actors/comprenew|Comprenew]] collected over 6,000 pounds of electronics at each event for recycling or refurbishment; and the newly formed [[initiatives/move-out-ann-arbor-coalition|Move Out Ann Arbor coalition]] diverted approximately 34 tons of material from local landfills during University of Michigan student move-out. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Technical emissions work also became more visible. OSI received EPA funding for a small food retailer refrigerant management program targeting at least 75 businesses, with a stated goal of reducing refrigeration emissions by 13,074.9 metric tons of CO2e. OSI and Urban Forestry also formalized a partnership with Urban Ashes to reuse urban forest waste, which the report describes as the first municipal wood waste recycling program in Michigan. Public Works received support to hire a new worker to expand commercial recycling and continued developing a strategy to expand compost services throughout the city. Year-six priorities named in the report include creating a circular economy strategy co-designed with a wide variety of Ann Arbor residents and launching a sustainable food strategy. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
