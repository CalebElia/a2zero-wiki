## Foundation

Strategy 2 of CAP-2020 targets a combined reduction of community-wide greenhouse gas emissions by 23% at a cost of just over $143,000,000, comprising six named actions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 31). The dominant mechanism is electrification of fossil-fuel-powered equipment, appliances, and vehicles, enabled by a transition to 100% renewable electricity. The six constituent actions are: Promote [[initiatives/home-and-business-electrification|Home & Business Electrification]], [[initiatives/electrify-buses|Electrify Buses]], [[initiatives/community-ev-solar-bulk-buy|Support Community Electric Vehicle and Solar Bulk Buys]], [[initiatives/electrify-city-fleet|Electrify City Fleet]], Electrify Private Fleet, and [[initiatives/expand-ev-charging-infrastructure|Expand Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 31).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 2 had been framed as foundational work: building resident-facing advisory services, contractor capacity, fleet electrification, and neighborhood-scale geothermal pilots. Year three included design and piloting of the [[initiatives/home-energy-advisor-program|A2Zero Home Energy Advisor Program]] with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] and [[actors/ecoworks|EcoWorks]], the first annual [[initiatives/electrification-expo|Electrification Expo]], contractor badging and education with [[actors/michigan-saves|Michigan Saves]], launch of the Heating Franchise Initiative, a U.S. Department of Energy planning grant for district geothermal in Bryant, and progress on municipal fleet electrification and curbside EV charging. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])

In year four, that groundwork expanded into a broader implementation platform. The city reached 25% electrification of its light-duty fleet vehicles and aimed for 50% by the end of calendar year 2024. It purchased the State of Michigan's first all-electric refuse trucks, procured two e-cargo bikes for parks operations and four additional e-bikes for the general fleet, and received a state grant for an electric forklift. OSI also hosted the second annual Home Electrification Expo, co-hosted a meeting with the [[actors/michigan-energy-innovation-business-council|Michigan Energy Innovation Business Council]] on Michigan's rollout of federal home rebates, and supported the gas leaf blower phase-out ordinance through a website, educational materials, and personalized outreach. OSI initiated a three-year contract with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] to design and launch the [[initiatives/home-energy-rebate-program|Home Energy Rebate Program]], continued contractor education around beneficial electrification, actively implemented the city's sustainability review as part of the plan review process, and assisted the [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]] in designing pathways to full decarbonization for multiple buildings. In Bryant, the district geothermal system moved into design through a DOE grant, and the city also initiated a citywide networked geothermal study and continued work on a new heating franchise focused on equitable, affordable, and reliable decarbonization of heating and cooling. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

Year five turns several of those designed supports into active public programs. More than 650 rebates had been claimed through [[initiatives/home-energy-rebate-program|Ann Arbor's Home Energy Rebates]], which cover weatherization, air sealing, heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, induction stoves, heat pump dryers, batteries, electrical upgrades, and e-bikes. The city also launched the [[initiatives/a2zero-heat-pump-concierge|A2ZERO Heat Pump Concierge]], which helps residents obtain system design, pricing estimates, rebate integration, contractor coordination, and installation oversight for replacing furnaces with heat pumps. OSI also continued actively implementing the city's sustainability review as part of the plan review process, hosted the third annual Home Electrification Expo, and continued contractor education around beneficial electrification, including expansion of the contractor badging program with [[actors/michigan-saves|Michigan Saves]]. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Transportation electrification also became more operational. The city reports an over 40% EV adoption rate in its light-duty fleet and nearly 30% electrification across the total city fleet. OSI launched a commercial EV charger discount program for commercial and multifamily property owners, offering up to four free Level 2 chargers plus a five-year software package, five-year warranty, and needed accessories. The city also won a $2.8 million Charging and Fueling Infrastructure grant through the Federal Highway Administration to expand public EV charging, with hiring underway for an EV coordinator to administer the work over four years. The electric lawn care rebate program launched with support from Grainger Industrial Supply and WEINGARTZ to help residents and landscaping companies transition away from gas-powered equipment in line with the city's leaf blower ordinance. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Fleet and transit electrification continued to broaden the strategy beyond private buildings. The city purchased two more electric refuse trucks, bringing the total on order to four, and the first electric refuse truck began providing service in spring 2025; OSI also received grant support through the [[actors/american-lung-association|American Lung Association]] for partial support on two of the side-load Mack trucks. On April 22, Ann Arbor and DTE signed a heating franchise that the report describes as the first in the country to include climate commitments and dedicated resources to address the climate crisis, including an Agreement Regarding Climate Action and a two-year work plan. OSI also supported [[actors/ann-arbor-area-transit-authority|TheRide]] in its successful $25 million [[actors/federal-transit-administration|Federal Transit Administration]] No Emission Bus Grant application for zero-emission buses and refueling stations. Together, these year-five updates show electrification maturing into a multi-sector delivery system spanning homes, fleets, charging, lawn equipment, and transit. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
