## Foundation

Strategy 2 of CAP-2020 targets a combined reduction of community-wide greenhouse gas emissions by 23% at a cost of just over $143,000,000, comprising six named actions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 31). The dominant mechanism is electrification of fossil-fuel-powered equipment, appliances, and vehicles, enabled by a transition to 100% renewable electricity. The six constituent actions are: Promote [[initiatives/home-and-business-electrification|Home & Business Electrification]], Electrify Buses, [[initiatives/community-ev-solar-bulk-buy|Support Community Electric Vehicle and Solar Bulk Buys]], Electrify City Fleet, Electrify Private Fleet, and [[initiatives/expand-ev-charging-infrastructure|Expand Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 31).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 2 had been framed as foundational work: building resident-facing advisory services, contractor capacity, fleet electrification, and neighborhood-scale geothermal pilots. Year three included design and piloting of the [[initiatives/home-energy-advisor-program|A2Zero Home Energy Advisor Program]] with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] and [[actors/ecoworks|EcoWorks]], the first annual [[initiatives/electrification-expo|Electrification Expo]], contractor badging and education with [[actors/michigan-saves|Michigan Saves]], launch of the [[initiatives/heating-franchise|Heating Franchise Initiative]], a U.S. Department of Energy planning grant for district geothermal in Bryant, and progress on municipal fleet electrification and curbside EV charging (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])).

In year four, that groundwork expanded into a broader implementation platform. The city reached 25% electrification of its light-duty fleet vehicles and aimed for 50% by the end of calendar year 2024. It purchased the [[actors/state-of-michigan|State of Michigan]]'s first all-electric refuse trucks, procured two e-cargo bikes for parks operations and four additional e-bikes for the general fleet, and received a state grant for an electric forklift. OSI also hosted the second annual [[initiatives/electrification-expo|Home Electrification Expo]], co-hosted a meeting with the [[actors/michigan-energy-innovation-business-council|Michigan Energy Innovation Business Council]] on Michigan's rollout of federal home rebates, and supported the gas leaf blower phase-out ordinance through a website, educational materials, and personalized outreach, with an electric yard-equipment bulk-buy planned for late 2024 (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

Resident and building electrification support became more concrete through program design and institutional partnerships. OSI initiated a three-year contract with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] to design and launch the [[initiatives/home-energy-rebate-program|Home Energy Rebate Program]] for building electrification, efficiency, and e-bikes, with launch planned for summer 2024. It continued contractor education around beneficial electrification, including expansion of the [[actors/michigan-saves|Michigan Saves]] contractor badging program, and actively implemented the city's sustainability review as part of the plan review process. OSI also assisted the [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]] in designing pathways to full decarbonization for multiple existing and new buildings, including support for solar, geothermal, electric appliances, and energy-efficiency improvements at multiple sites. Additional AAHC-related work included support for geothermal at [[locations/121-catherine-street|121 Catherine Street]], EV charger installation, and launch of AAHC's first EV car-share program (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

Thermal and transportation infrastructure remained central. In Bryant, the [[initiatives/geothermal-districts|district geothermal system]] moved into design through a DOE grant and was scoped to cover at least 75% of heating and cooling load for 262 households, the county mental health facility, [[locations/bryant-elementary-school|Bryant Elementary School]], the [[locations/bryant-neighborhood-community-center|Bryant Community Center]], and the city's public works facility. OSI also initiated a citywide networked geothermal study and continued work on a new [[initiatives/heating-franchise|heating franchise]] focused on equitable, affordable, and reliable decarbonization of heating and cooling. On transportation electrification, the city installed and began operating Michigan's first curbside public Level 2 chargers, added chargers at four city park sites with one more upcoming, continued implementing the EV-readiness ordinance, designed a charger discount program for multifamily and commercial property owners, and submitted a grant to deploy chargers at roughly two dozen more locations. OSI also supported [[actors/ann-arbor-area-transit-authority|TheRide]]'s zero-emissions propulsion study and grant application. Year five priorities include deploying 60 more EV chargers and launching an electric vehicle bulk-buy program (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).
