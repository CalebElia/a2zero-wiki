## Foundation

Strategy 6 of CAP-2020 targets a combined 0.1% reduction in community-wide greenhouse gas emissions at a total cost of approximately $7,500,000 [Source: Page 99] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The dominant mechanism is community resilience-building rather than direct emissions reduction, with the strategy explicitly noting that reducing greenhouse gas emissions was not the primary selection criterion for its actions. The strategy comprises six named actions: Invest in [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|Resilience Hubs]], Foster Neighborhood and Youth Ambassador Programs, [[initiatives/preserve-and-enhance-local-tree-canopy|Preserve and Enhance the Local Tree Canopy]], [[initiatives/asset-and-needs-mapping-of-neighborhoods|Conduct Asset and Needs Mapping of Neighborhoods]], [[initiatives/emergency-preparedness-kits|Assist in Assembling and Distributing Emergency Preparedness Kits]], and [[initiatives/environmental-sensors-monitoring|Implement Sensors to Monitor and Manage Heat, Air Quality, Waterways, and Flooding]] [Source: Page 99] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 6 had already established resilience hubs, tree-canopy expansion, and environmental monitoring as core implementation areas. Year three included completion of the second resilience hub at the [[locations/bryant-neighborhood-community-center|Bryant Neighborhood Community Center]], operational use of the [[initiatives/northside-resilience-hub|Northside Community Center Resilience Hub]] during a February ice storm, an EPA grant application to expand a countywide resilience network, growth of the [[initiatives/10000-trees-initiative|10,000 Trees Initiative]] to roughly 5,750 trees, expansion of the air-quality monitoring network to four sensors, launch of a public air-data platform, and growth of the [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]] to 85 members. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])

In year four, resilience expanded from a city-site model toward a broader neighborhood and regional network. The city won a $1,000,000 U.S. EPA Environmental Justice Government-to-Government grant to launch the [[initiatives/model-regional-resilience-network|Model Regional Resilience Network]] and expand resilience hubs in [[locations/ann-arbor|Ann Arbor]] and across [[actors/washtenaw-county|Washtenaw County]]. [[locations/peace-neighborhood-center|Peace Neighborhood Center]] and the [[locations/green-baxter-court-community-center|Green Baxter Court Community Center]] were identified as the next two resilience hub sites, with initial engagement and planning underway. Backup power and distributed energy became more central through purchase of a [[technology/mobile-nanogrid|mobile nanogrid]], pilot battery-backup Solarize bulk buys, increased solar-and-storage capacity at the Bryant Community Center, and battery backup at the [[locations/ann-arbor-senior-center|Ann Arbor Senior Center]]. Natural systems and preparedness programs also advanced through tree planting, canopy-analysis funding, air-quality monitoring expansion, pollinator-yard-care growth, and emergency-kit distribution and preparedness seminars. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

Year five shows resilience becoming more distributed, more regional, and more tightly linked to energy infrastructure. OSI officially trained its 100th A2ZERO Ambassador with graduation of the sixth cohort, bringing the total number trained to 118, and the seventh cohort began training in early 2025 with graduation expected to bring the total to 148. The report also says the [[technology/sesame-solar-unit|Sesame Solar Unit]] is coming to the [[locations/ann-arbor|Ann Arbor]] community as a [[technology/mobile-nanogrid|mobile nanogrid]] for sustainability education and backup power at central city facilities. Household preparedness continued to develop as a repeatable program model: in partnership with the [[actors/office-of-emergency-management|Office of Emergency Management]], OSI led a preparedness seminar at [[actors/washtenaw-community-college|Washtenaw Community College]] that distributed 30 emergency kits, hosted another seminar at [[actors/avalon-housing|Avalon Housing]] that distributed 20 kits, and distributed preparedness information and raffled five family emergency kits during A2ZERO Week ward parties. As part of the Cinema and Sustainability series, OSI also hosted a showing of COOKED: Survival by Zip Code followed by a panel discussion on community resilience and climate adaptation. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Regional coordination became more visible as OSI co-hosted multiple [[initiatives/washtenaw-county-resilience-network|Washtenaw County Resilience Network]] meetings with the [[actors/washtenaw-county-resilience-office|Washtenaw County Resilience Office]], bringing together critical service providers to discuss resilience initiatives and partnerships. OSI also hosted a breakout session at the 2025 Ann Arbor Climate Teach-In on the role of resilience hubs in the climate transition. At the same time, the strategy faced funding instability: OSI was accepted into the Center for Heat Resilient Communities 2025 cohort to address extreme heat, but that funding was terminated, and the city's $1,000,000 EPA Environmental Justice grant to start a [[initiatives/model-regional-resilience-network|model regional resilience network]] and expand resilience hubs was also terminated, with the city actively disputing the termination. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Resilience hubs themselves are moving from designation toward site upgrades and procurement. In partnership with the [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]], OSI issued an RFP for installation of solar and battery storage at Green Baxter Court Resilience Hub. At [[locations/peace-neighborhood-center|Peace Neighborhood Center]], a [[technology/led-lighting|LED lighting]] retrofit and HVAC controls installation were scheduled to improve efficiency and free up resources for programming; OSI also supported preparation for future solar and energy storage and helped integrate sustainability into summer STEAM programming. Natural systems and monitoring continued to scale as well: 50 additional sign-ups brought the [[initiatives/pollinator-aware-yard-care|Pollinator-Aware Yard Care program]] to over 450 participants, eight additional air-quality monitors brought the network total to 10, and over 2,250 additional trees were distributed through the 10,000 Trees program, bringing the total to more than 11,000 after the city reached the 10,000-tree milestone in September 2024. The year-five resilience story is therefore one of widening reach across preparedness, hubs, monitoring, and regional coordination, even as some federal support became less reliable. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
