## Foundation

The "Other Actions" strategy in CAP-2020 covers programs and activities that do not fit into other strategy areas, plus greenhouse gas emissions [[initiatives/carbon-offsets|offsets]] to close remaining gaps toward community-wide carbon neutrality. Combined, its four actions are estimated to achieve just under 14% of community-wide emissions reductions at a cost of just under $6,000,000 [Source: Page 113] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The dominant mechanism for closing the emissions gap is the purchase and retirement of carbon offsets (including renewable energy credits), supplemented by an internal carbon price applied across City Service Areas. The four named actions comprising this strategy are: [[initiatives/advance-equity-programs|Advance Equity Programs]], Promote [[initiatives/sustaining-ann-arbor-together-grants|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together Grant Program]], [[initiatives/internal-carbon-price|Establish Internal Carbon Price]], and [[initiatives/offset-greenhouse-gas-emissions|Offset Greenhouse Gas Emissions]] [Source: Page 113] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 7 had already become the connective infrastructure for A2ZERO implementation. Year three included passage of the 20-year [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage|Community Climate Action Millage]], repeat recognition on CDP's A List, Gold-level recognition from [[actors/michigan-green-communities|Michigan Green Communities]], growth of the [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]] to 85 members, an [[actors/americorps|AmeriCorps]] grant to support sustainability education, over $70,000 distributed through the [[initiatives/sustaining-ann-arbor-together-grants|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together grant program]], expansion of the [[initiatives/a2zero-collaborators-network|A2Zero Collaborators Network]] to more than 120 organizations, and extensive staff participation in equity, regional, and national climate-learning networks. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])

In year four, the city expanded both public-facing engagement tools and internal implementation capacity. OSI launched the [[initiatives/a2zero-dashboard|A2ZERO Dashboard]] to show progress on implementing the plan, launched the [[initiatives/ann-arbor-climate-corps|Ann Arbor Climate Corps]] as a national AmeriCorps program focused on sustainability education and behavior change, formalized the Sustaining Ann Arbor Together grant program, and distributed $80,000 through it. The A2ZERO Community Collaborators network expanded to more than 130 organizations, and OSI reported hosting more than 100 community meetings, events, presentations, or discussions during the year. Communications infrastructure also became more deliberate and multi-channel through The Charging Station newsletter, social media, Green Light on CTN, a recurring radio segment on 107.1, Earth Day collaboration with [[actors/leslie-science-and-nature-center|Leslie Science and Nature Center]], Green Fair and A2ZERO Week, and a Sustainability Forum series. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

Year five confirms Strategy 7 as the operating layer that makes the rest of A2ZERO visible, staffed, and socially legible. Fourteen AmeriCorps members served with the Ann Arbor Climate Corps; eight completed full terms, serving 13,600 hours and earning Segal Education Awards totaling $55,109. More than 500 residents helped celebrate A2ZERO Week through 30 events co-hosted with more than 15 community organizations, 510 community members attended events throughout the week, 400 visited the OSI tent at Ann Arbor SummerFest, and 540 people signed up for the Zero Waste pledge. AmeriCorps members also tabled at the Wednesday Farmers Market, engaging 418 community members, and conducted a fall door-knocking campaign that distributed information on city rebates and Inflation Reduction Act incentives to more than 1,000 households. OSI reported hosting more than 200 discrete community events over the year. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

The strategy also remained deeply place-based and collaborative. AmeriCorps members and OSI staff assisted the Bryant Community Center with its annual neighborhood cleanup day, helping 60 households with recycling and neighborhood beautification, and helped engage Bryant residents around home energy assessment sign-ups, funding opportunities, and community charrette sessions for neighborhood geothermal planning. In collaboration with Leslie Science and Nature Center and [[actors/neutral-zone|Neutral Zone]], OSI helped create [[initiatives/youth-now-climate-action|Youth Now Climate Action]], a small group of high school students from [[actors/ann-arbor-public-schools|Ann Arbor Public Schools]] focused on environmental problems and solutions. OSI also formalized a collaboration with the [[actors/pittsfield-village-community|Pittsfield Village community]] to explore neighborhood decarbonization, held an in-person meeting of the A2ZERO Community Collaborators Network with over 30 collaborators attending, and piloted [[initiatives/seu-open-houses|SEU open houses]] in residents' homes to help neighbors learn about the Sustainable Energy Utility. The resilience team also supported food distribution at [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|resilience hubs]] throughout the city to avoid service disruptions. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Institutional credibility and communications reach continued to expand. OSI received the "Best of the Midwest" award from the Climate Action Museum; Ann Arbor received an A score from CDP and became one of two cities to achieve Platinum status in Michigan Green Communities. OSI hosted the first [[initiatives/cinema-and-sustainability-film-series|Cinema and Sustainability film series]] at the [[locations/michigan-theater|Michigan Theater]], continued Sustainability Forums at the downtown branch of the Ann Arbor District Library, filmed nine episodes of Green Light on CTN with a combined total of 2,000 views, grew its social media accounts by over 750 followers across Instagram and Facebook, and added 320 subscribers to The Charging Station for a total of 4,502 with a 46% average open rate. The city also continued submitting testimony in multiple [[actors/michigan-public-service-commission|Michigan Public Service Commission]] cases and reported that cumulative city interventions over four years had saved ratepayers more than $1,000,000,000. Additional year-five engagement highlights included collaboration with Leslie Science and Nature Center on an annual Earth Day celebration, hosting a delegation from sister city Tuebingen, Germany focused exclusively on carbon neutrality, continued weekly radio segments on 107.1, nearly 100 media interviews, more than 50 conference or webinar presentations, and staff service on advisory boards including the UM Museum of Natural History and the Refugee Agricultural Partnership Program. The report's year-six priorities also point toward continued engagement expansion through a storytelling program, greater collaboration with community collaborators, and a sustainability-focused "Welcome to Ann Arbor" initiative. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
