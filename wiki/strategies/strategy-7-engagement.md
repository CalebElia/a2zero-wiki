## Foundation

The "Other Actions" strategy in CAP-2020 covers programs and activities that do not fit into other strategy areas, plus greenhouse gas emissions offsets to close remaining gaps toward community-wide carbon neutrality. Combined, its four actions are estimated to achieve just under 14% of community-wide emissions reductions at a cost of just under $6,000,000 [Source: Page 113] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The dominant mechanism for closing the emissions gap is the purchase and retirement of carbon offsets (including renewable energy credits), supplemented by an internal carbon price applied across City Service Areas. The four named actions comprising this strategy are: Advance Equity Programs, Promote [[initiatives/sustaining-ann-arbor-together-grant-program|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together Grant Program]], Establish Internal Carbon Price, and Offset Greenhouse Gas Emissions [Source: Page 113] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 7 had already become the connective infrastructure for A2ZERO implementation. Year three included passage of the 20-year [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage|Community Climate Action Millage]], repeat recognition on CDP's A List, Gold-level recognition from Michigan Green Communities, growth of the [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]] to 85 members, an [[actors/americorps|AmeriCorps]] grant to support sustainability education, over $70,000 distributed through the [[initiatives/sa2t-grant-program|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together grant program]], expansion of the [[initiatives/a2zero-collaborators-network|A2Zero Collaborators Network]] to more than 120 organizations, and extensive staff participation in equity, regional, and national climate-learning networks (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])).

In year four, the city expanded both public-facing engagement tools and internal implementation capacity. OSI launched the [[initiatives/a2zero-dashboard|A2ZERO Dashboard]] to show progress on implementing the plan, launched the [[initiatives/ann-arbor-climate-corps|Ann Arbor Climate Corps]] as a national AmeriCorps program focused on sustainability education and behavior change, formalized the [[initiatives/sa2t-grant-program|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together grant program]], and distributed $80,000 through it. The [[initiatives/a2zero-collaborators-network|A2ZERO Community Collaborators network]] expanded to more than 130 organizations, and OSI reported hosting more than 100 community meetings, events, presentations, or discussions during the year (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

Communications infrastructure became more deliberate and multi-channel. OSI restructured The Charging Station newsletter to provide more accessible and actionable information, developed a strategic communication plan and branding guidelines, launched OSI social media channels on Instagram and Facebook, began airing Green Light on CTN, and started a recurring sustainability segment on local radio station 107.1. It also collaborated with the [[actors/leslie-science-and-nature-center|Leslie Science and Nature Center]] on an annual Earth Day celebration, hosted Green Fair and A2ZERO weeks, and ran a Sustainability Forum series on building electrification, the Home Energy Rating Disclosure ordinance, e-waste, equity in action, and new residential energy programs. The report also notes a planned monthly storytelling series beginning in July 2024 to profile how local climate efforts are affecting people's lives (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

The strategy also remained tied to equity, institutional credibility, and regional learning. Ann Arbor was recognized by [[actors/cdp|CDP]] as one of 119 global climate action leaders on the 2023 A List, received mitigation and adaptation badges from the [[actors/global-covenant-of-mayors-for-climate-and-energy|Global Covenant of Mayors for Climate and Energy]], and earned the highest-level Gold award from [[actors/michigan-green-communities|Michigan Green Communities]]. Staff participated in an Energy Equity Community of Practice, Champions for Change, and the United Way's 21-day equity challenge; presented at more than 30 conferences or national webinars; and served on advisory boards and peer-learning groups. The report also highlights partnership with a [[actors/university-of-michigan|University of Michigan]] science communications class, work to update the city's Capital Improvements Planning Process to better integrate sustainability criteria, and continued exploration of a regional climate collaborative. Staff obtained Home Energy Score certification and BPI Total Home Performance certification, while [[actors/julie-roth|Julie Roth]] and [[actors/missy-stults|Missy Stults]] received named professional recognitions. Year five priorities include a youth ambassador program, deeper engagement with underrepresented communities, a "Welcome to Ann Arbor" sustainability initiative with local realtors, and expanded collaboration with community partners (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).
