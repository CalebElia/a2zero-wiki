## Foundation

Strategy 3 of CAP-2020 targets a 13.4% reduction in community-wide greenhouse gas emissions at a cost of just over $14,500,000, combining 11 named actions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 45). The dominant mechanisms are building energy efficiency improvements and policy tools such as updated building codes, rental licensing requirements, and group-buy procurement programs. The 11 constituent actions are: (1) [[initiatives/transition-to-more-energy-efficient-homes-and-businesses|Transition to More Energy Efficient Homes and Businesses]], (2) [[initiatives/update-building-codes|Update Building Codes]], (3) [[initiatives/led-street-lighting-and-traffic-signals|Power Street Lighting and Traffic Signals with LED]], (4) [[initiatives/benchmark-and-disclose-energy-usage|Benchmark and Disclose Energy Usage]], (5) [[initiatives/loan-loss-reserve|Establish Loan Loss Reserve]], (6) Develop [[initiatives/energy-concierge-and-community-engagement-program|Energy Concierge and Community Engagement Program]], (7) [[initiatives/affordable-housing-net-zero-energy|Transition Affordable Housing Sites to Net Zero Energy]], (8) Promote [[initiatives/green-rental-housing|Green Rental Housing Program]], (9) Launch [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Green Business Challenge]], (10) [[initiatives/aging-in-place-efficiently|Support Aging in Place Efficiently]], and (11) [[initiatives/expand-weatherization-program|Expand Weatherization Program]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 45).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 3 had already combined regulation, low-income support, and municipal retrofits. Year three included implementation of the first commercial energy and water benchmarking ordinance in Michigan, drafting of a [[initiatives/home-energy-rating-disclosure-program|Home Energy Rating Disclosure]] policy, promotion of stronger state building codes, integration of an A2Zero sustainability review into plan review, grants for housing-provider efficiency improvements, the [[initiatives/aging-in-place-efficiently|Aging in Place Efficiently]] pilot, Bryant neighborhood decarbonization funding, federal support for [[initiatives/net-zero-energy-affordable-housing|net-zero energy affordable housing]], municipal decarbonization audits, and grant-funded LED streetlight conversion planning (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])).

In year four, these efforts became a more complete implementation stack. The free [[initiatives/energy-concierge-and-community-engagement-program|A2ZERO Home Energy Advisor]] service launched for all city residents. OSI launched Home Energy Rating Disclosure, hired two assessors to complete Home Energy Scores, and became a [[actors/us-department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] Home Energy Score Partner, enabling OSI to train and certify assessors to support ordinance implementation. On the larger-building side, the city continued implementing the commercial and multifamily energy and water benchmarking ordinance, with all buildings over 20,000 square feet now required to track and report annual energy and water use (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

Affordability and implementation support also expanded. OSI initiated a three-year contract with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] to design and launch the [[initiatives/home-energy-rebate-program|Home Energy Rebate Program]], and authorized over $4,000,000 for residential rebates over the coming 2.5 years. Weatherization services were expanded through a grant to the [[actors/washtenaw-county-office-of-community-and-economic-development|Washtenaw County Office of Community and Economic Development]] to help design support programs for income-qualified residents to fully decarbonize their homes. In Bryant, OSI and [[actors/community-action-network|Community Action Network]] secured over $1,300,000 for neighborhood decarbonization while continuing engagement and fundraising, and launched a three-year contract with CAN to support that work (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).

Municipal and commercial-building work deepened as well. OSI joined DOE's Better Buildings Coalition, launched the [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Ann Arbor Green Business Challenge]] with [[actors/ann-arbor-spark|SPARK]]—a program open to anyone in the city—and entered a three-year contract with the [[actors/ann-arbor-washtenaw-2030-district|Ann Arbor / Washtenaw 2030 District]] to support benchmarking, commercial energy-saving programs, reporting, and on-demand decarbonization assistance. OSI staff were also appointed to the Washtenaw 2030 District Board of Directors. Decarbonization assessments were completed at six municipal facilities, work began to support decarbonization of the new election center, funding was secured to transition all streetlights to LED, and an LED retrofit was completed at [[locations/wheeler-service-center|Wheeler Service Center]]. The [[initiatives/green-rental-housing-program|Green Rental Housing program]] made significant progress and was expected to go to Council in summer or fall 2024, while OSI continued pushing for updates to Michigan building and energy codes and advanced workforce-development conversations with [[actors/ibew-252|IBEW 252]] and [[actors/ua-local-190|UA 190]], especially around geothermal deployment. Year five priorities include achieving the LED street-lighting goal and creating a weatherization expansion program for income-qualified households (([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])).
