## Foundation

Strategy 3 of CAP-2020 targets a 13.4% reduction in community-wide greenhouse gas emissions at a cost of just over $14,500,000, combining 11 named actions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 45). The dominant mechanisms are building energy efficiency improvements and policy tools such as updated building codes, rental licensing requirements, and group-buy procurement programs. The 11 constituent actions are: (1) [[initiatives/transition-to-more-energy-efficient-homes-and-businesses|Transition to More Energy Efficient Homes and Businesses]], (2) [[initiatives/update-building-codes|Update Building Codes]], (3) [[initiatives/led-street-lighting-and-traffic-signals|Power Street Lighting and Traffic Signals with LED]], (4) [[initiatives/benchmark-and-disclose-energy-usage|Benchmark and Disclose Energy Usage]], (5) [[initiatives/loan-loss-reserve|Establish Loan Loss Reserve]], (6) Develop [[initiatives/energy-concierge-and-community-engagement-program|Energy Concierge and Community Engagement Program]], (7) [[initiatives/affordable-housing-net-zero-energy|Transition Affordable Housing Sites to Net Zero Energy]], (8) Promote [[initiatives/green-rental-housing-program|Green Rental Housing Program]], (9) Launch [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Green Business Challenge]], (10) [[initiatives/aging-in-place-efficiently|Support Aging in Place Efficiently]], and (11) [[initiatives/expand-weatherization-program|Expand Weatherization Program]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]], Page 45).

## Progress Synthesis

Before year four, Strategy 3 had already combined regulation, low-income support, and municipal retrofits. Year three included implementation of the first commercial energy and water benchmarking ordinance in [[locations/michigan|Michigan]], drafting of a Home Energy Rating Disclosure policy, promotion of stronger state building codes, integration of an A2Zero sustainability review into plan review, grants for housing-provider efficiency improvements, the Aging in Place Efficiently pilot, Bryant neighborhood decarbonization funding, federal support for net-zero energy affordable housing, municipal decarbonization audits, and grant-funded LED streetlight conversion planning. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]])

In year four, these efforts became a more complete implementation stack. The free A2ZERO Home Energy Advisor service launched for all city residents. OSI launched Home Energy Rating Disclosure, hired two assessors to complete Home Energy Scores, and became a Department of Energy Home Energy Score Partner, enabling OSI to train and certify assessors to support ordinance implementation. On the larger-building side, the city continued implementing the commercial and multifamily energy and water benchmarking ordinance, with all buildings over 20,000 square feet now required to track and report annual energy and water use. OSI also initiated a three-year contract with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] to design and launch the [[initiatives/home-energy-rebate-program|Home Energy Rebate Program]], expanded weatherization services through a grant to the [[actors/washtenaw-county-office-of-community-and-economic-development|Washtenaw County Office of Community and Economic Development]], and deepened commercial-building work through the [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Ann Arbor Green Business Challenge]] and a three-year contract with the [[actors/ann-arbor-washtenaw-2030-district|Ann Arbor / Washtenaw 2030 District]]. The [[initiatives/green-rental-housing-program|Green Rental Housing program]] also made significant progress and was expected to go to Council in summer or fall 2024. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

Year five shows the building-efficiency strategy becoming more legible as a layered delivery system. The A2ZERO Home Energy Advisor had helped over 300 residents obtain free home energy assessments with customized home decarbonization plans, and the [[initiatives/home-energy-rebate-program|Home Energy Rebate program]] provided hundreds of thousands of dollars to homeowners, renters, property managers, and landlords for efficiency improvements. In collaboration with A2ZERO Ambassadors, the city also hosted its first net zero energy home tour during A2ZERO Week, giving residents a chance to learn directly from a neighbor about sustainability practices in action; the report says the event's success led OSI to offer it again on June 1 as part of 2025 A2ZERO Week. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Regulatory and market-shaping tools also advanced. After years of engagement, the city brought forward the [[initiatives/green-rental-housing-program|Green Rental Housing ordinance]] for Council consideration as a proposed minimum baseline for health, comfort, and energy efficiency in rental housing. Implementation of the Energy and Water Benchmarking Ordinance continued in earnest, with major effort centered on bringing buildings into compliance; through collaboration with the 2030 District, buildings over 20,000 square feet are benchmarking and learning about practices that could save money and improve health and safety. The Home Energy Rating Disclosure effort also moved further into implementation, with 700 homes receiving a completed Home Energy Score through the city. At the state level, Michigan adopted updated building codes in early 2025, though the report notes the updates do not yet apply to the residential code. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

Bryant remains the clearest place-based expression of the strategy. OSI provided funding to the county's Office of Community and Economic Development so it could expand weatherization and decarbonization offerings for eligible households, secured a $3.1 million [[actors/michigan-public-service-commission|Michigan Public Service Commission]] grant to improve energy efficiency and electrify homes in the Bryant neighborhood, and released an RFP for contractors to help make sustainability and energy-related improvements there. In partnership with [[actors/community-action-network|Community Action Network]], OSI also secured continuing operational support from the [[actors/mcknight-foundation|McKnight Foundation]] for Bryant neighborhood decarbonization work. Commercial and institutional efficiency work gained more structure as well: the [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Green Business Challenge]], launched in 2024, added individualized work plans through [[actors/fresh-coast-climate-solutions|Fresh Coast Climate Solutions]] and hired four interns to expand outreach; OSI reported growing collaboration with the 2030 District around benchmarking, the commercial solar program, and sustainability in houses of worship; and municipal and affordable-housing work continued through LED streetlight conversion, support for [[actors/avalon-housing|Avalon Housing]]'s Stimson Apartments, and funding support for the [[actors/ann-arbor-housing-commission|Ann Arbor Housing Commission]] project at [[locations/121-catherine-street|121 Catherine Street]]. Year-six priorities named in the report include scaling the [[initiatives/green-business-challenge|Green Business Challenge]] to 50 local businesses, creating a new [[initiatives/green-rental-housing-program|Green Rental Housing program]], implementing a multifamily rebate program, and securing funding to scale Aging in Place Efficiently. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])
