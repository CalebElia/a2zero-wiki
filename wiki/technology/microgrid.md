Ann Arbor is pursuing microgrid infrastructure through a FEMA BRIC grant application for [[locations/fire-station-3|Fire Station 3]] and [[locations/veterans-memorial-park|Veterans Memorial Park]]. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) The year-four report also describes resilience-focused solar and battery upgrades at [[locations/bryant-neighborhood-community-center|Bryant Community Center]] that can provide three days of power with no grid connection, showing how local distributed energy systems are being used to support community-serving facilities during outages. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

In Year Five, OSI drafted community choice aggregation legislation and supported microgrid-enabling legislation for consideration by the State House and State Senate, extending the technology's use beyond individual facility deployments into state policy advocacy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
