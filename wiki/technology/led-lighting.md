LED lighting is being deployed by the City of Ann Arbor to replace conventional streetlights and traffic signals, with energy savings of up to 70% for streetlights and 85% for traffic signals compared to conventional technology ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). A key barrier is that approximately two-thirds of Ann Arbor's streetlights are currently owned by [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]], requiring the City to negotiate and purchase these assets before conversion can proceed ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The initiative targets replacement of 4,467 streetlights and all traffic signals, with full conversion projected to be complete by 2029 at a total cost of $3,100,000 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The projected GHG reduction potential is 2,600 MTCO²e, representing 0.1% of community-wide emissions ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

In Year Three, the City secured a $980,000 SEMCOG Carbon Reduction Program grant for LED streetlight conversions across the City, starting in fall 2023, along with a separate $200,000 SEMCOG grant and a $25,000 State of Michigan grant that funded an LED lighting conversion at [[locations/wheeler-center|Wheeler Service Center]] ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, the City secured grant funding and began actively transitioning all streetlights to LED, completed the LED lighting retrofit project at Wheeler Service Center, and joined the U.S. Department of Energy's Better Buildings Coalition to help promote building decarbonization strategies nationally ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Five, the [[locations/peace-neighborhood-center|Peace Neighborhood Center]] Resilience Hub scheduled an LED lighting retrofit alongside HVAC control installation to improve building energy efficiency ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
