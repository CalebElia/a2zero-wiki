District geothermal systems use shared ground loop infrastructure to provide heating and cooling to multiple buildings, achieving greater efficiency than individual installations ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The A2Zero plan identified geothermal districts as a potential energy efficiency and electrification strategy for Ann Arbor, though no specific sites or implementation details were specified in this section ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

Ann Arbor's first real-world application of this technology is in the [[locations/bryant-neighborhood|Bryant neighborhood]]. In Year Three, the City won a $580,000 U.S. Department of Energy Community Geothermal Planning Grant to design a district geothermal system there ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, design work began on the system, targeting at least 75% of the heating and cooling load for 262 households along with the County Mental Health facility and Bryant Elementary School ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Five, OSI and a team of collaborators won $10,000,000 from the U.S. Department of Energy to deploy this networked geothermal system, projected — in conjunction with solar and weatherization — to reduce residents' energy costs by 77% and carbon emissions by 75% ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
