## Conflicting claims

**CAP-2020 (the original plan):** "By the end of 2023, a 24MW solar installation is fully operational at the former Ann Arbor landfill and on the land held in PUD with Pittsfield Township." ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) This figure is preserved in [[strategies/strategy-1-renewable-grid|Strategy 1's]] frozen Foundation section as CAP-2020's original design intent.

**Year 1:** "Conducted studies on the feasibility and cost of a 24MW solar installation on a capped landfill." ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]) Consistent with CAP-2020's original target.

**Year 2 onward:** "Final design for a 20MW solar field on our capped landfill... completed." ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) Years 3-4 consistently refer to a "20MW solar installation" or "20MW solar field," with no mention of 24MW. Year 4 secures a $5,000,000 award for the project while "continuing to strategize" on its development. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]])

**Year 5:** Makes no mention of the project's capacity at all — no confirmation of either figure, and no report of groundbreaking or construction. ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]])

## The discrepancy

CAP-2020's original plan and the Year 1 feasibility study both target 24MW. Starting in Year 2, every report consistently states 20MW instead — a 4MW reduction that is never explained in any of the five sources. Possible explanations, none confirmed by the source record:
- The Year 1 feasibility study evaluated 24MW as an upper bound; 20MW was chosen as the optimized final design based on interconnection limits, cost, or site capacity constraints.
- A phase of the project was deferred or removed between the feasibility study and final design.
- The two figures refer to slightly different site footprints (landfill only vs. landfill plus the Pittsfield Township PUD land CAP-2020 explicitly named).

## Why it matters

[[strategies/strategy-1-renewable-grid|Strategy 1]] targets 78 MW of onsite renewable generation by 2030. A 4MW difference in this single project is about 5% of that entire citywide target — not a rounding error. The project has real momentum ($5M secured, Year 4) but no confirmed final capacity as of the most recent source (Year 5). Any assessment of progress toward the 78MW goal that cites this project's capacity is currently choosing between two real, sourced, unreconciled numbers without saying so.

## Best-guess explanation

The 20MW figure has been consistent across four consecutive annual reports (Years 2-5, three of which explicitly restate it), suggesting it is the authoritative, current project size rather than a one-off typo. The Year 1 24MW figure most plausibly reflects an early feasibility-study upper bound that was right-sized during final design — but no source confirms this explanation, and Year 5's total silence on the project's capacity means the discrepancy remains genuinely open, not just historically resolved. Track future sources for a construction contract or groundbreaking announcement naming a specific capacity — that would be the definitive number.
