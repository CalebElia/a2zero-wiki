[[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] is the primary electric utility supplying electricity to Ann Arbor and surrounding communities, and it remains the incumbent provider whose generation mix strongly shapes the city's community-wide emissions profile and renewable energy strategy and against which several A2Zero renewable-grid pathways are being evaluated; it also serves as the existing utility provider under A2Zero Strategy 1 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In 2022, over 54% of DTE Electric's fuel mix used to supply Ann Arbor was generated using coal, while 13% was generated from renewables and an additional 2% was purchased as renewable energy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In 2023, over 50% of DTE Electric's fuel mix used to supply electricity in Ann Arbor was generated using coal, while 13% came from renewables, continuing the utility fuel-mix context that underlies Ann Arbor's pursuit of alternatives such as [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|Community Choice Aggregation]], the [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]], and municipalization analysis ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Michigan state law requires DTE to meet a 15% renewable portfolio standard ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).

DTE is a named collaborator on both the Community Solar Program and the [[initiatives/landfill-solar-project|Landfill Solar Project]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Under the [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|CCA]] model, DTE would continue to provide transmission and distribution services even as the City procures power from an alternative supplier ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). For the Landfill Solar Project, DTE is a co-developer alongside the City and Pittsfield Township, with grid upgrade costs at the point of intersection still to be determined ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

The City of Ann Arbor has intervened in multiple Michigan Public Service Commission cases involving DTE, including U-20836, to challenge rate increases and protect the interests of residents with solar installations, as well as distributed generation policies affecting Ann Arbor residents and local clean energy deployment ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Four, the city also submitted testimony in multiple Michigan Public Service Commission cases concerning reliability, rates, programs, and service, and launched public comment on a Phase II municipalization study to conduct a detailed valuation of DTE's electric assets in Ann Arbor ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).
