[[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] is Ann Arbor's incumbent electric utility and also the gas utility whose fuel mix, rates, and infrastructure remain central to the city's community-wide emissions profile and renewable-energy transition planning; it is the primary electric utility supplying electricity to Ann Arbor and surrounding communities, and it serves as the existing utility provider under A2Zero Strategy 1 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] remains the utility context against which Ann Arbor is pursuing local renewable-energy alternatives such as [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|Community Choice Aggregation]], the [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]], and municipalization analysis; Year Five reinforces the city's movement toward supplemental local renewable supply through the voter-authorized SEU without adding new fuel-mix or regulatory details about DTE ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).

In 2022, over 54% of DTE Electric's fuel mix used to supply Ann Arbor was generated using coal, while 13% was generated from renewables and an additional 2% was purchased as renewable energy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In 2023, over 50% of DTE Electric's fuel mix used to supply electricity in Ann Arbor was generated using coal, while 13% came from renewables ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Michigan state law requires DTE to meet a 15% renewable portfolio standard ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).

DTE is a named collaborator on both the Community Solar Program and the [[initiatives/landfill-solar-project|Landfill Solar Project]] ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). Under the [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|CCA]] model, DTE would continue to provide transmission and distribution services even as the City procures power from an alternative supplier ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). For the Landfill Solar Project, DTE is a co-developer alongside the City and Pittsfield Township, with grid upgrade costs at the point of intersection still to be determined ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

The City of Ann Arbor has intervened in multiple Michigan Public Service Commission cases involving DTE, including U-20836, to challenge rate increases and protect the interests of residents with solar installations, as well as distributed generation policies affecting Ann Arbor residents and local clean energy deployment ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]) ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Four, the city also submitted testimony in multiple Michigan Public Service Commission cases concerning reliability, rates, programs, and service, and launched public comment on a Phase II municipalization study to conduct a detailed valuation of DTE's electric assets in Ann Arbor ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Five, OSI reported intervening for the first time in two gas cases while calling for a "Future of Gas" proceeding to outline a path toward decarbonized heating and cooling across DTE's service territory ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).

On April 22, Ann Arbor and DTE signed a heating franchise that the report describes as the first in the country to include climate commitments, an Agreement Regarding Climate Action, and a two-year work plan specifying joint climate actions ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
