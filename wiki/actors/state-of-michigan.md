The State of Michigan is a required collaborator for the [[initiatives/update-building-codes|Update Building Codes]] initiative because Michigan municipalities are constrained by the State's building code and cannot independently adopt more stringent local standards ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). Ann Arbor's A2Zero plan calls for working with the State to adopt the 2021 building code and zero code appendix, with the State expected to adopt the updated code in 2021 and provide educational materials and inspector training ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

As a broader governmental partner in Ann Arbor's A2Zero climate action efforts ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]), the State was also identified as a collaborator in the deployment of 50 additional EV chargers as part of A2Zero's Year 3 priorities ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]).

Like the [[actors/city-of-ann-arbor|City of Ann Arbor]], the State of Michigan is a governmental umbrella: what "the State" does in A2Zero's sources is carried out through specific agencies, each with its own page.

## State agencies

- [[actors/michigan-department-of-transportation|Michigan Department of Transportation (MDOT)]] — permitting authority and partner for Ann Arbor transit, bike infrastructure, and roadway projects.
- [[actors/michigan-egle|Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE)]] — grant funder of A2Zero electrification and infrastructure programs.
- [[actors/michigan-public-service-commission|Michigan Public Service Commission (MPSC)]] — regulates Michigan's investor-owned utilities; the venue for the City's multi-year rate-case advocacy and grant funding relationship with the state.

## The State of Michigan as an actor (not delegated to an agency)

In late 2023, Governor Gretchen Whitmer signed SB 271 into law, requiring the State of Michigan to generate 50% of its electricity from clean energy sources by 2030 and 100% by 2040 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]) — building on the State's existing 15% renewable portfolio standard for utilities ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In early 2025, the State adopted updated building codes following years of advocacy, resolving the code-adoption goal set out in A2Zero's original design; residential code updates remain pending ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). The Michigan legislature also passed, and Governor Whitmer signed, legislation prohibiting homeowners associations from restricting residents' access to solar panels, heat pumps, clotheslines, and other energy efficiency measures ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). The State has also provided direct funding support to Ann Arbor's electrification and infrastructure programs, including grants for EV charging, LED lighting conversion, and electric vehicle purchases ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]], [[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).
