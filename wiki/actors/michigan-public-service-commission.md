The Michigan Public Service Commission (MPSC) is the state regulatory body overseeing Michigan's investor-owned electric and gas utilities, including [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]]. The City of Ann Arbor has intervened before the MPSC across multiple years to advance A2ZERO goals on two tracks: rate-case advocacy and direct grant funding.

Beginning with case U-20836, the city challenged a DTE Energy proposed rate increase and changes to net-metering compensation for residential solar customers, undertaken in part to protect residents who had participated in the [[initiatives/solarize-ann-arbor|Ann Arbor Solarize Program]] ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). In Year Three, the city submitted testimony in U-20836 objecting to the rate increase and challenging proposed distributed-generation rates, and intervened in three additional cases — U-21172, U-21297, and U-21376 — on renewable energy opportunities, rate reduction, and reliability; OSI staff also participated in MPSC working groups, technical conferences, and stakeholder meetings on reliability and distributed energy resources ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). Year Four testimony continued in multiple MPSC cases tying local grid-decarbonization goals to rate equity and distributed-generation access ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Five, the city intervened for the first time in two gas cases before the MPSC, calling for a "Future of Gas" proceeding, and reported that cumulative city interventions over four years had saved ratepayers more than $1,000,000,000 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).

Separately from rate-case advocacy, the MPSC awarded a $3.1 million grant funding the [[funding-events/bryant-neighborhood-mpsc-grant-2025|Bryant Neighborhood MPSC Grant 2025]] to improve energy efficiency and electrify homes in the Bryant neighborhood ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
