The Community Action Network is a nonprofit partner that collaborated with the City of Ann Arbor's Office of Sustainability and Innovations on the [[initiatives/bryant-neighborhood-decarbonization|Bryant Neighborhood Decarbonization]] initiative, which aims to create the first fully decarbonized existing neighborhood in America ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). During Year One of A2ZERO, the partnership focused on co-creating a whole home health, safety, comfort, and decarbonization assessment with Bryant neighborhood residents ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). The initiative has since expanded to include collaboration with [[actors/elevate|Elevate]] and the [[actors/university-of-michigan|University of Michigan]] ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). The Community Action Network is also listed as a collaborator and project co-designer for Ann Arbor's [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|Neighborhood and Youth Ambassador Programs]] under A2Zero Strategy 6 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]).

CAN's role in Bryant neighborhood decarbonization scaled substantially across Years 3–5: in Year Three, the partnership won $500,000 to help 19 households make health, safety, and quality-of-life improvements ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]); in Year Four, it secured an additional $1,300,000 and the City launched a three-year contract with CAN specifically to support the decarbonization project ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]); and by Year Five, CAN helped OSI secure continuing operational support from the McKnight Foundation for the project ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). Separately, CAN has collaborated with the City, Church of the Good Shepherd, Ann Arbor Public Schools, and ZeroWaste.org on Neighborhood Swap Days, reusing more than 6,800 items ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]).
