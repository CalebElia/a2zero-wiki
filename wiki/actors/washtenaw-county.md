[[actors/washtenaw-county|Washtenaw County]] was one of 67 organizations that signed on as an A2Zero partner by early March 2020, agreeing to co-host events, share information with its networks, and display the A2Zero partner logo ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). Washtenaw County coordinated with [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] and Ann Arbor Public Schools to complete greenhouse gas emissions inventories in Year 3 ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). The County also provided a $4,000 Waste Reduction Sponsorship to advance the A2R3 returnable containers program ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). The City applied for an EPA grant to expand resilience hubs throughout Washtenaw County as part of a broader county-wide resilience network ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]).

The County's [[actors/washtenaw-county-office-of-community-and-economic-development|Office of Community and Economic Development]] received expanded weatherization services grant funding to design new support programs helping income-qualified residents fully decarbonize their homes ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year 5, OSI co-hosted multiple [[initiatives/washtenaw-county-resilience-network|Washtenaw County Resilience Network]] meetings with the County's own [[actors/washtenaw-county-resilience-office|Resilience Office]], convening service providers from across the county to coordinate climate resilience efforts ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]). A $1,000,000 EPA Environmental Justice Government-to-Government grant intended to launch a Model Regional Resilience Network and expand Resilience Hubs across the City and Washtenaw County (see [[funding-events/us-epa-environmental-justice-government-to-government-grant-2024]]) had its funding terminated in Year 5; the City is actively disputing the termination ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
