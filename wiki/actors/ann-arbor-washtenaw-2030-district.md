The [[actors/ann-arbor-washtenaw-2030-district|Ann Arbor 2030 District]] is listed as a collaborator on the [[initiatives/expand-improve-local-transit|Expand and Improve Local Transit]] and [[initiatives/tiered-parking-rates|Establish Tiered Parking Rates]] initiatives under A2Zero Strategy 4 ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). The 2030 District's involvement reflects its role in coordinating sustainability efforts among commercial property owners and businesses in the Ann Arbor area ([[sources/cap/cap-2020|cap-2020]]). As a nonprofit organization, the 2030 District collaborated with the City of Ann Arbor on the design of a pilot commercial Solarize program to help businesses access solar energy ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]), and subsequently partnered with [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] to launch the Commercial Solar Program in Year Three of A2ZERO, aimed at incentivizing commercial solar adoption in Washtenaw County ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, [[actors/office-of-sustainability-and-innovations|OSI]] staff were appointed to the [[actors/ann-arbor-washtenaw-2030-district|Ann Arbor / Washtenaw 2030 District]] Board of Directors, and the district was described as supporting commercial, multifamily, and nonprofit buildings in understanding and improving their energy performance ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). In Year Five, the district's staff and collaborators supported implementation of the Energy and Water Benchmarking Ordinance, helping buildings over 20,000 square feet come into compliance; OSI also described a deepening relationship with the district that let OSI reach more businesses, architects, property managers, and designers than in prior years, including collaboration on the commercial solar program and sustainability outreach to houses of worship ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
