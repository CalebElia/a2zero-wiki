The Bryant neighborhood in Ann Arbor was selected as the site for a neighborhood-scale decarbonization initiative during Year One of A2ZERO, in partnership with the Community Action Network ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). The initiative focuses on co-creating whole home health, safety, comfort, and decarbonization assessments with residents ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year1|a2zero-year1]]). The broader effort, involving Bryant residents, the Community Action Network, Elevate, and the University of Michigan, aims to make Bryant the first fully decarbonized existing neighborhood in America ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]).

Bryant served as the initial pilot site for the Whole-Home Decarbonization Assessment in Year Two of A2ZERO, with plans to expand the program city-wide in Year Three ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). The neighborhood is the site of the [[initiatives/bryant-neighborhood-decarbonization|Bryant Neighborhood Decarbonization Program]], which carried Year Three priorities including growing community support and fundraising ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). During Year Two, the City also applied for a federal grant specifically to support climate action work in this neighborhood ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]). Funding for work in Bryant includes grants from the U.S. EPA, the McKnight Foundation, and Solar Moonshot ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year2|a2zero-year2]]).

In Year Three, the City won a planning grant from the U.S. Department of Energy to design a district geothermal loop in the Bryant neighborhood, and secured a $500,000 MI-HOPE grant to support the neighborhood's decarbonization work; a Year Four priority named in the Year Three report was to continue growing momentum and funding for the program, including helping 20 homes become fully carbon neutral ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year3|a2zero-year3]]). In Year Four, design work began on the district geothermal system funded by that DOE planning grant, targeting at least 75% of the heating and cooling load for 262 households along with the County Mental Health facility and Bryant Elementary School; in partnership with the [[actors/community-action-network|Community Action Network]], the City also secured over $1,300,000 for the Bryant decarbonization project and launched a three-year contract with Community Action Network to support it, while a stated Year Five priority was to win a federal grant to implement at least a portion of the geothermal system ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year4|a2zero-year4]]). That priority was met in Year Five, when OSI and a team of collaborators won $10,000,000 from the U.S. Department of Energy to deploy a networked geothermal system in the Bryant neighborhood, projected — in conjunction with solar and weatherization — to reduce residents' energy costs by 77% and carbon emissions by 75%; separately, OSI secured a $3.1 million grant from the Michigan Public Service Commission to improve energy efficiency and electrify homes in Bryant, with foundational work underway so the program can begin making improvements in Year Six, and the [[actors/mcknight-foundation|McKnight Foundation]] continued its operational support for the Bryant decarbonization project in partnership with Community Action Network ([[sources/annual-reports/a2zero-year5|a2zero-year5]]).
