## Cross-strategy synthesis

[[strategies/strategy-1-renewable-grid|Strategy 1: 100% Renewable Energy Grid]] began with a strong emphasis on [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|Community Choice Aggregation]] and broader local solar and storage concepts, but over time the work appears to have shifted toward more incremental, buildable pathways. Across the A2Zero reporting years, progress is most visible through [[initiatives/solarize-ann-arbor|Solarize Ann Arbor]], [[initiatives/onsite-renewables-bulk-buy|Onsite Renewables Bulk Buy]], [[initiatives/community-solar-pilot|Community Solar Pilot]], [[initiatives/wheeler-center-solar-park|Wheeler Center Solar Park]], and continued exploration of a [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]]. [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] remains a central actor because utility partnership, interconnection, and broader regulatory conditions continue to shape what can actually be deployed. The overall arc is one of moving from a transformational utility-structure vision toward a portfolio of local renewable projects and enabling mechanisms that can advance despite unresolved state and grid constraints.

[[strategies/strategy-2-electrification|Strategy 2: Building Electrification]] has evolved from a planning-oriented strategy into a more implementation-focused portfolio spanning homes, businesses, fleets, buses, and charging infrastructure. The clearest signs of maturation are programs like [[initiatives/a2zero-heat-pump-concierge|A2Zero Heat Pump Concierge]], [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]], [[initiatives/expand-ev-charging-infrastructure|Expand EV Charging Infrastructure]], and the paired efforts to [[initiatives/electrify-city-fleet|Electrify City Fleet]] and [[initiatives/electrify-buses|Electrify Buses]]. Over the five-year arc, the strategy appears to have broadened from identifying electrification targets to building the customer support, incentives, and public infrastructure needed for adoption. Its remaining challenge is scale: replacing fossil equipment quickly enough while ensuring charging access and electric capacity keep pace.

[[strategies/strategy-3-building-efficiency|Strategy 3: Building Energy Efficiency]] shows one of the clearest transitions from framework design into a diversified implementation portfolio. Early concepts around [[initiatives/update-building-codes|Update Building Codes]] and [[initiatives/benchmark-and-disclose-energy-usage|Benchmark and Disclose Energy Usage]] were joined over time by more resident- and building-facing efforts such as [[initiatives/energy-concierge-and-community-engagement-program|Energy Concierge and Community Engagement Program]], [[initiatives/expand-weatherization-program|Expand Weatherization Program]], [[initiatives/green-rental-housing-program|Green Rental Housing]], and [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]]. The strategy’s year-over-year arc suggests increasing attention to existing-building retrofits, rental housing, and affordability rather than relying only on future code improvements. It now functions as a core delivery strategy for household decarbonization, even as questions remain about mandatory requirements, financing tools, and measured emissions outcomes.

[[strategies/strategy-4-vmt-reduction|Strategy 4: Vehicle Miles Traveled Reduction]] broadened from a set of transportation-demand and land-use levers into a wider implementation agenda touching mobility, transit, zoning, and parking. Key threads include [[initiatives/non-motorized-transportation-plan|Non-Motorized Transportation Plan]], [[initiatives/expand-improve-local-transit|Expand and Improve Local Transit]], [[initiatives/expand-improve-regional-transit|Expand and Improve Regional Transit]], [[initiatives/transit-oriented-development-zoning|Transit-Oriented Development Zoning]], and [[initiatives/tiered-parking-rates|Tiered Parking Rates]]. Over time, the strategy appears to have become more integrated with housing and development policy through [[initiatives/increase-diversity-of-housing|Increase Diversity of Housing]] and [[initiatives/develop-mixed-use-neighborhoods|Develop Mixed-Use Neighborhoods]], reflecting the city’s recognition that VMT is shaped by urban form as much as by transportation service. Even so, the available record emphasizes implementation activity more than quantified citywide VMT reduction.

[[strategies/strategy-5-materials-waste|Strategy 5: Materials & Waste]] expanded from a relatively bounded waste-reduction concept into a broader circular-economy and zero-waste portfolio. Core efforts such as [[initiatives/composting-expansion|Composting Expansion]], [[initiatives/commercial-recycling|Commercial Recycling]], [[initiatives/move-toward-circular-economy|Move Toward Circular Economy]], [[initiatives/enhance-refrigerant-recycling-reuse-program|Enhance Refrigerant Recycling and Reuse Program]], and later [[initiatives/zero-waste-plan|Zero Waste Plan]] and [[initiatives/circular-economy-strategy|Circular Economy Strategy]] suggest a widening scope over time. The year-over-year arc points to increasing engagement with businesses, food systems, reuse, and procurement-related questions rather than focusing only on residential diversion. What remains less clear is which of these efforts produced the largest measurable greenhouse-gas reductions and whether sustainable materials requirements became formalized.

[[strategies/strategy-6-resilience|Strategy 6: Community Resilience]] moved steadily from conceptual resilience actions into visible place-based and neighborhood-facing implementation. The strongest markers are [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|Resilience Hubs]], [[initiatives/northside-resilience-hub|Northside Resilience Hub]], [[initiatives/10000-trees-initiative|10,000 Trees Initiative]], [[initiatives/preserve-and-enhance-local-tree-canopy|Preserve and Enhance Local Tree Canopy]], [[initiatives/environmental-sensors-monitoring|Environmental Sensors Monitoring]], and [[initiatives/emergency-preparedness-kits|Emergency Preparedness Kits]]. Across the reporting period, the strategy appears to have become more institutionalized, with named hub projects, tree-canopy programs, and neighborhood mapping efforts giving it a clearer operational footprint. Its central tension is that resilience benefits are real but harder to quantify than emissions reductions, especially when long-term operating models for hubs and microgrids are still emerging.

[[strategies/strategy-7-engagement|Strategy 7: Community Engagement & Equity]] has shifted from plan-level enabling concepts into a sustained infrastructure for participation, grants, and neighborhood-centered climate action. Programs such as [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]], [[initiatives/a2zero-collaborators-network|A2Zero Collaborators Network]], [[initiatives/sustaining-ann-arbor-together-grants|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together Grants]], [[initiatives/sa2t-grant-program|SA2T Grant Program]], and [[initiatives/deepen-community-engagement-underrepresented-communities|Deepen Community Engagement with Underrepresented Communities]] indicate that engagement became an ongoing delivery mechanism rather than a one-time outreach function. Over the five-year arc, equity appears to have become more embedded in how programs are targeted and funded, even as earlier concepts like [[initiatives/internal-carbon-price|Internal Carbon Price]] and [[initiatives/offset-greenhouse-gas-emissions|Offset Greenhouse Gas Emissions]] recede from prominence. The strategy now serves as connective tissue across the rest of A2Zero, translating climate goals into community participation, legitimacy, and access.

### Connections

Several initiatives clearly span multiple strategies and act as bridges between technical decarbonization and community delivery. [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]] links renewable energy, electrification, efficiency, and equity by turning funding into household-scale action, while [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]] and [[initiatives/grid-capacity-expansion-electrification|Grid Capacity Expansion for Electrification]] connect the success of Strategies 1 and 2 directly to utility and infrastructure constraints. Land-use and mobility work in [[initiatives/transit-oriented-development-zoning|Transit-Oriented Development Zoning]], [[initiatives/increase-diversity-of-housing|Increase Diversity of Housing]], and [[initiatives/develop-mixed-use-neighborhoods|Develop Mixed-Use Neighborhoods]] shows how Strategy 4 depends on planning choices that also shape building emissions and equity outcomes. Resilience and engagement are similarly intertwined through [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]], neighborhood-focused programs, and tree or hub investments that deliver both adaptation and community trust. Across the portfolio, the recurring pattern is that progress in one strategy often enables another, but utility limits, financing design, and implementation capacity can also become shared bottlenecks.
