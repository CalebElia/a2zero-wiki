## Cross-strategy synthesis

[[strategies/strategy-1-renewable-grid|Strategy 1: 100% Renewable Energy Grid]] has evolved from an early emphasis on state-enabled [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|Community Choice Aggregation]] toward a more incremental portfolio of local generation and financing pathways. Across the A2Zero reporting years, visible progress clustered around [[initiatives/solarize-ann-arbor|Solarize Ann Arbor]], [[initiatives/onsite-renewables-bulk-buy|Onsite Renewables Bulk Buy]], [[initiatives/community-solar-pilot|Community Solar Pilot]], and municipal-scale projects like [[initiatives/wheeler-center-solar-park|Wheeler Center Solar Park]], while [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]] remained a recurring exploration track. [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] is the central external actor because interconnection, grid capacity, and utility partnership conditions shape what can actually be built. The overall arc is one of pragmatic local deployment under persistent regulatory and infrastructure constraints rather than a clean transition to a city-controlled renewable supply model.

[[strategies/strategy-2-electrification|Strategy 2: Building Electrification]] moved from planning for broad fuel-switching to a more implementation-oriented mix of household, fleet, and charging programs. Over time, the strategy became more tangible through [[initiatives/home-and-business-electrification|Home and Business Electrification]], [[initiatives/a2zero-heat-pump-concierge|A2Zero Heat Pump Concierge]], [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]], and transportation-side efforts such as [[initiatives/electrify-city-fleet|Electrify City Fleet]], [[initiatives/electrify-buses|Electrify Buses]], and [[initiatives/expand-ev-charging-infrastructure|Expand EV Charging Infrastructure]]. The year-over-year pattern suggests widening delivery capacity and more direct incentives, even as scale depends on customer uptake and equipment turnover cycles. Its unresolved challenge is that electrification success increasingly depends on upstream grid readiness and coordination with renewable supply efforts.

[[strategies/strategy-3-building-efficiency|Strategy 3: Building Energy Efficiency]] broadened from a code-and-program design agenda into one of the city’s deepest implementation portfolios. The strategy now spans [[initiatives/transition-to-more-energy-efficient-homes-and-businesses|Transition to More Energy Efficient Homes and Businesses]], [[initiatives/energy-concierge-and-community-engagement-program|Energy Concierge and Community Engagement Program]], [[initiatives/expand-weatherization-program|Expand Weatherization Program]], [[initiatives/benchmark-and-disclose-energy-usage|Benchmark and Disclose Energy Usage]], [[initiatives/green-rental-housing|Green Rental Housing]], and [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]]. Over successive annual reports, the emphasis appears to have shifted from designing standards and support tools to delivering retrofit assistance across owner-occupied, rental, commercial, and affordable housing contexts. Even so, the wiki record still leaves open how much of this portfolio became mandatory, how financing tools matured, and what measured emissions reductions can be attributed to each program.

[[strategies/strategy-4-vmt-reduction|Strategy 4: Vehicle Miles Traveled Reduction]] expanded from a set of transportation and land-use levers into a broader implementation space linking mobility, zoning, and neighborhood form. Core threads include [[initiatives/non-motorized-transportation-plan|Non-Motorized Transportation Plan]], [[initiatives/expand-improve-local-transit|Expand and Improve Local Transit]], [[initiatives/expand-improve-regional-transit|Expand and Improve Regional Transit]], [[initiatives/transit-oriented-development-zoning|Transit-Oriented Development Zoning]], [[initiatives/develop-mixed-use-neighborhoods|Develop Mixed-Use Neighborhoods]], and [[initiatives/tiered-parking-rates|Tiered Parking Rates]]. The year-over-year arc suggests real movement on active transportation, transit access, and development reform, but without a clearly documented citywide VMT trend in the source set. As a result, this strategy reads as structurally important and highly connected to planning decisions, yet less legible than others in terms of quantified outcomes.

[[strategies/strategy-5-materials-waste|Strategy 5: Materials & Waste]] developed from an initial waste-reduction concept into a broader zero-waste and circular-economy portfolio. Its implementation landscape includes [[initiatives/composting-expansion|Composting Expansion]], [[initiatives/commercial-recycling|Commercial Recycling]], [[initiatives/move-toward-circular-economy|Move Toward Circular Economy]], [[initiatives/zero-waste-plan|Zero Waste Plan]], [[initiatives/circular-economy-strategy|Circular Economy Strategy]], and targeted efforts like [[initiatives/enhance-refrigerant-recycling-reuse-program|Enhance Refrigerant Recycling Reuse Program]] and [[initiatives/support-plant-rich-diet|Support Plant-Rich Diet]]. Over time, the strategy appears to have diversified toward business-facing, food-system, and reuse interventions rather than remaining narrowly focused on curbside waste streams. The main gap is not activity but measurement: the current record does not surface a single consistent year-over-year metric tying these efforts to emissions reductions.

[[strategies/strategy-6-resilience|Strategy 6: Community Resilience]] shows one of the clearest transitions from conceptual planning to place-based implementation. Through the A2Zero years, work coalesced around [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|Resilience Hubs]], [[initiatives/northside-resilience-hub|Northside Resilience Hub]], [[initiatives/preserve-and-enhance-local-tree-canopy|Preserve and Enhance Local Tree Canopy]], [[initiatives/10000-trees-initiative|10,000 Trees Initiative]], [[initiatives/asset-and-needs-mapping-of-neighborhoods|Asset and Needs Mapping of Neighborhoods]], and [[initiatives/environmental-sensors-monitoring|Environmental Sensors Monitoring]]. The strategy’s arc suggests increasing institutionalization, with named hubs, tree programs, and neighborhood preparedness efforts becoming more durable parts of the city’s climate response. Its open questions center on long-term operating models, equity targeting, and how resilience benefits are being tracked alongside emissions outcomes.

[[strategies/strategy-7-engagement|Strategy 7: Community Engagement & Equity]] broadened from plan-level equity commitments and financing concepts into a sustained community-facing infrastructure for participation. Key vehicles include [[initiatives/advance-equity-programs|Advance Equity Programs]], [[initiatives/sustaining-ann-arbor-together-grants|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together Grants]], [[initiatives/sa2t-grant-program|SA2T Grant Program]], [[initiatives/deepen-community-engagement-underrepresented-communities|Deepen Community Engagement Underrepresented Communities]], [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]], and [[initiatives/a2zero-collaborators-network|A2Zero Collaborators Network]]. Across the reporting arc, the strategy appears to have shifted away from abstract tools like [[initiatives/internal-carbon-price|Internal Carbon Price]] and [[initiatives/offset-greenhouse-gas-emissions|Offset Greenhouse Gas Emissions]] toward neighborhood-scale engagement, grants, and relationship-building. That makes it foundational to implementation across the whole plan, even if the causal link between engagement activities and emissions reductions remains difficult to quantify.

### Connections

Several initiatives function as connective tissue across the plan, especially [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]], which links electrification, efficiency, and equity-oriented delivery. [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]] and [[initiatives/grid-capacity-expansion-electrification|Grid Capacity Expansion for Electrification]] tie [[strategies/strategy-1-renewable-grid|renewable grid]] ambitions directly to [[strategies/strategy-2-electrification|electrification]] demand, making utility and infrastructure constraints a shared bottleneck; in that sense, [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] influences more than Strategy 1 alone. Land-use and mobility initiatives such as [[initiatives/transit-oriented-development-zoning|Transit-Oriented Development Zoning]], [[initiatives/develop-mixed-use-neighborhoods|Develop Mixed-Use Neighborhoods]], and [[initiatives/increase-diversity-of-housing|Increase Diversity of Housing]] connect VMT reduction to housing, resilience, and equitable access outcomes. [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2Zero Ambassadors Program]] spans resilience and engagement, while tree canopy, affordable housing, and neighborhood-focused programs show how adaptation and decarbonization are often delivered together. Across the wiki, the strongest pattern is that enabling programs in engagement, financing, and grid capacity determine how quickly the more technical strategies can scale.
