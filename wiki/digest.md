## Cross-strategy synthesis

Strategy 1 has remained anchored in [[initiatives/community-choice-aggregation|Community Choice Aggregation]] as the original organizing concept, but the portfolio has widened over time into a more mixed set of procurement, local generation, and enabling efforts. Across the first five A2ZERO years, work appears to have moved from pathway-setting toward implementation through [[initiatives/community-solar-pilot|Community Solar Pilot]], [[initiatives/landfill-solar-project|Landfill Solar Project]], [[initiatives/commercial-solar-program|Commercial Solar Program]], and the exploratory [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]]. [[actors/dte-energy|DTE Energy]] remains the central external actor because utility interconnection, procurement structure, and grid readiness shape what the city can actually deliver. The overall arc is one of diversification and groundwork rather than a completed transition to a 100% renewable grid.

Strategy 2 has evolved from a broad electrification agenda into a more visible implementation portfolio spanning buildings, vehicles, and charging. The clearest movement is through [[initiatives/home-and-business-electrification|Home and Business Electrification]], [[initiatives/a2zero-heat-pump-concierge|A2ZERO Heat Pump Concierge]], [[initiatives/electrify-buses|Electrify Buses]], [[initiatives/electrify-city-fleet|Electrify City Fleet]], and [[initiatives/expand-ev-charging-infrastructure|Expand EV Charging Infrastructure]]. Year over year, the strategy appears to have added practical adoption supports and incentives, especially through [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]], rather than relying only on long-range commitments. Its main unresolved constraint is that electrification progress depends on affordability, charging deployment, and the grid upgrades needed to absorb new electric load.

Strategy 3 shows one of the clearest shifts from policy design into household- and building-level delivery. Early building-efficiency intent has broadened into implementation through [[initiatives/transition-to-more-energy-efficient-homes-and-businesses|Transition to More Energy-Efficient Homes and Businesses]], [[initiatives/energy-concierge-and-community-engagement-program|Energy Concierge and Community Engagement Program]], [[initiatives/benchmark-and-disclose-energy-usage|Benchmark and Disclose Energy Usage]], [[initiatives/expand-weatherization-program|Expand Weatherization Program]], and [[initiatives/affordable-housing-net-zero-energy|Affordable Housing Net Zero Energy]]. The year-over-year arc suggests increasing attention to financing and access, including rebates and tools like [[initiatives/loan-loss-reserve|Loan Loss Reserve]], even if scale and mandatory requirements remain unclear. This strategy has become a practical bridge between emissions reduction, housing quality, and equity outcomes.

Strategy 4 has broadened from a classic transportation-demand framework into a more integrated mobility and land-use agenda. The core pillars remain [[initiatives/non-motorized-transportation-plan|Non-Motorized Transportation Plan]], [[initiatives/expand-improve-local-transit|Expand and Improve Local Transit]], [[initiatives/expand-improve-regional-transit|Expand and Improve Regional Transit]], [[initiatives/tiered-parking-rates|Tiered Parking Rates]], and [[initiatives/transit-oriented-development-zoning|Transit-Oriented Development Zoning]], but later years point toward implementation through bike infrastructure, mobility hubs, and pass-based transit access. The strategy’s arc is notable for tying emissions reduction to urban form via [[initiatives/develop-mixed-use-neighborhoods|Develop Mixed-Use Neighborhoods]] and [[initiatives/increase-diversity-of-housing|Increase Diversity of Housing]]. Even so, the available record emphasizes program buildout more than measured VMT reduction results.

Strategy 5 has expanded from a waste-diversion frame into a broader circular-economy and consumption strategy. Implementation appears to have grown through [[initiatives/composting-expansion|Composting Expansion]], [[initiatives/commercial-recycling|Commercial Recycling]], [[initiatives/zero-waste-plan|Zero Waste Plan]], [[initiatives/circular-economy-strategy|Circular Economy Strategy]], and food-related efforts such as [[initiatives/support-plant-rich-diet|Support Plant-Rich Diet]]. The year-over-year pattern suggests increasing operational and behavioral programming, but with less clear quantitative reporting than in some energy-focused strategies. As a result, this strategy reads as conceptually broadening faster than its measurable outcomes are being documented.

Strategy 6 has developed from a resilience framework into a set of place-based projects with visible neighborhood presence. The strongest through-lines are [[initiatives/resilience-hubs|Resilience Hubs]], [[initiatives/preserve-and-enhance-local-tree-canopy|Preserve and Enhance Local Tree Canopy]], [[initiatives/asset-and-needs-mapping-of-neighborhoods|Asset and Needs Mapping of Neighborhoods]], [[initiatives/environmental-sensors-monitoring|Environmental Sensors Monitoring]], [[initiatives/10000-trees-initiative|10,000 Trees Initiative]], and [[initiatives/decentralized-energy-microgrids|Decentralized Energy Microgrids]]. Over the first five years, the strategy appears to have moved from planning categories into concrete pilots such as hub development, AQMesh deployment, tree planting, and youth/community engagement through [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2ZERO Ambassadors Program]]. Its trajectory is increasingly spatial and equity-sensitive, though the scale of operational resilience gains is still hard to quantify from the current record.

Strategy 7 has become the connective tissue for participation, equity, and community legitimacy across A2ZERO. What began with equity commitments, [[initiatives/sustaining-ann-arbor-together-grants|Sustaining Ann Arbor Together Grants]], [[initiatives/sa2t-grant-program|SA2T Grant Program]], [[initiatives/internal-carbon-price|Internal Carbon Price]], and [[initiatives/offset-greenhouse-gas-emissions|Offset Greenhouse Gas Emissions]] has broadened into durable engagement infrastructure through [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2ZERO Ambassadors Program]], [[initiatives/deepen-community-engagement-underrepresented-communities|Deepen Community Engagement with Underrepresented Communities]], and [[initiatives/neighborhood-resiliency-plans|Neighborhood Resiliency Plans]]. Year over year, the emphasis appears to have shifted toward direct community-facing programs and grant-supported participation, while pricing and offset mechanisms remained more aspirational or background design tools. This strategy’s accomplishment is less a single emissions outcome than the creation of channels through which other strategies can reach residents more effectively and more equitably.

### Connections

Several initiatives clearly span multiple strategies, especially [[initiatives/community-climate-action-millage-rebates|Community Climate Action Millage Rebates]], which help connect building efficiency, electrification, and equity-oriented participation. Grid and power-system work in [[initiatives/grid-capacity-expansion-electrification|Grid Capacity Expansion for Electrification]], [[initiatives/sustainable-energy-utility|Sustainable Energy Utility]], and [[initiatives/decentralized-energy-microgrids|Decentralized Energy Microgrids]] links renewable energy, electrification, and resilience, showing how progress in one area can enable or bottleneck another. Housing and land-use initiatives such as [[initiatives/increase-diversity-of-housing|Increase Diversity of Housing]], [[initiatives/transit-oriented-development-zoning|Transit-Oriented Development Zoning]], and [[initiatives/affordable-housing-net-zero-energy|Affordable Housing Net Zero Energy]] connect transportation, building emissions, and equity outcomes. Community-facing programs including [[initiatives/a2zero-ambassadors-program|A2ZERO Ambassadors Program]] and [[initiatives/neighborhood-resiliency-plans|Neighborhood Resiliency Plans]] help translate technical strategies into neighborhood adoption and resilience planning. Across the portfolio, the biggest recurring dependency is that local adoption programs can move only as fast as financing, utility coordination, and regulatory authority allow.
